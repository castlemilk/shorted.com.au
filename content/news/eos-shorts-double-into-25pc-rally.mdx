[Electro Optic Systems](/shorts/EOS) was **10.15%** shorted on **30 September** in ASIC's daily aggregate report: 22,506,069 of 221,702,162 shares on issue. [ref-1] On **1 July** it was **4.73%**, or 10,247,977 shares. [ref-2] The share count rose by 5,000,000 in early July; positions more than doubled.

About half of it came in September: 7.46% on **31 August**, [ref-16] 8.80% on **17 September** [ref-3] and 10.42% on **18 September**, [ref-4] a one-day step of 1.62 percentage points, or about 3.6 million shares. That was the month's heaviest session: 6.77 million shares traded, a $10.34 close, up 3.4%. The position peaked at 10.59% on **24 September** [ref-5] and eased to 10.15% by month-end.

The price rose too: $9.52 at the 4 September close, $11.94 on 5 October, up 25.4%, and $11.60 on 6 October. [Days to cover](/blog/days-to-cover-asx) sit at 9.6.

EOS's own news in the second half of September was two website items. On **24 September** it said the R400 remote weapon system is listed on the US Joint Interagency Task Force 401 Counter-UAS Marketplace, where eligible US government buyers can order "through an established US Army contracting mechanism"; no order is announced. [ref-7] On **25 September** it published a letter of intent with the Netherlands Ministry of Defence on its Apollo laser weapon: "subject to further work", the ministry intends to buy a number of systems, with no value or date. [ref-8] Motley Fool had the shares up 7.21% at $11.45 just after midday that day and noted the listing "doesn't represent a new contract". [ref-9] The close was $11.32.

Neither was an ASX lodgement; EOS's lodgements from 22 to 28 September, as listed on 7 October, were one unquoted-securities notification and three substantial-holder notices. [ref-10] Vanguard became a substantial holder on **18 September** with 5.672%, the day of the step. [ref-11] Citigroup became one on **23 September** with 5.1524% and ceased on **24 September**. [ref-12][ref-13] All four Citigroup entities in its notice hold the shares "subject to an obligation to return" under securities lending agreements, one also acting as agent lender; the notice does not say what the borrowed shares were used for, or whether any were sold short. [ref-12]

The half-year to 30 June, published on **25 August**, had revenue from continuing operations of $168.8m, up 283%, underlying EBITDA of $21.6m, a $33.7m loss after tax from continuing operations (including a $34.0m non-cash remeasurement tied to the MARSS acquisition), a contracted order book of about $846m ($170m a year earlier) and a 2026 full-year revenue outlook of $360m to $400m. [ref-6]

[DroneShield](/shorts/DRO), top of the [W40 report](/reports/weekly/10-most-shorted-asx-stocks-week-40-2026) and 15.07% short on 30 September [ref-1] with 15.1 days to cover, announced that day a three-year Domestic Shield IDIQ contract vehicle under the same task force: "a maximum contract value of up to $500 million" in its press release, [ref-14] "a ceiling value of US$500 million" in its ASX announcement, which adds that the contract "does not guarantee any orders". [ref-17]

On Shorted's [squeeze screen](/battlegrounds) EOS ranks first at 84.4, one of 43 securities where the price is rising while reported positions build. Both are Shorted-derived metrics, not official figures; a crowded position is not a [squeeze](/blog/short-squeeze-explained).

ASIC publishes the aggregate **T+4**; the reports "will not contain short seller details". [ref-15][ref-1] The position more than doubled and the price rose by a quarter; the report records both and explains neither.
