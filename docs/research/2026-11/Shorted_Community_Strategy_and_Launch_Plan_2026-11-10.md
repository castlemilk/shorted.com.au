# Shorted community strategy and launch plan
## Competing with HotCopper — 10 November 2026

**Recommendation:** build an ASX research community around the companies people already follow. Use Shorted's data, charts and ownership views to start useful conversations, and preserve the best contributions as research that improves over time.

Shorted can plausibly win a valuable segment of this market. The evidence does not establish that it can displace HotCopper's overall audience. The first commercial goal should be a small community that improves members' research and supports paid workflow tools. Expansion should follow demonstrated member-to-member value.

All product designs, recruitment numbers, budgets, thresholds and commercial scenarios below are proposals, not completed work or validated benchmarks.

## 1. The competitive reality

HotCopper already documents free membership, follow/ignore controls, configurable watchlists, per-post sentiment and held/not-held declarations. The position declarations are user-provided snapshots rather than brokerage verification. Its company-facing business markets enhanced forums, content production, email and display advertising, and investor insights. [S1–S4, S18–S19]

The principal strategic obstacle is the accumulated relationship network: contributors with an audience, readers who recognise familiar names, existing company conversations and an expectation of replies. This is our inference about the incumbent's advantage, not a measured switching-cost estimate.

HotCopper's advertising page attributes its headline traffic figures to average annual 2022–2023 Google Analytics data. Those figures should not be used as a current 2026 market-share estimate. The relevant commercial packages are enquiry-led on the reviewed page. [S4]

Strawman makes the field more demanding. It already combines collaborative company research, model portfolios, member meetings and contribution credits. Its current Premium article says applications are closed while free journals and model portfolios remain available. A current public membership price was not established. Its linked reward-method article dates from 2022. [S5–S6]

TradingView distributes ideas to followers of both an author and a ticker, preserves public ideas after an initial editing period, and supports subsequent updates. Stocktwits keeps basic participation free while selling deeper data, search and other capabilities. Seeking Alpha compensates contributors through a combination of eligible coverage bonuses and consumption by paid subscribers. [S7–S9]

| Platform | Established model | Lesson for Shorted |
|---|---|---|
| HotCopper | Company discussion and advertiser/issuer reach | People, reply expectations and familiar company context matter. Basic social controls are parity. |
| Strawman | Concentrated research club with portfolios and rewards | Contributor benefits and research history already have competitors. The workflow must be distinctive. |
| TradingView | Analysis created around a chart, distributed through authors and instruments | Let the research activity itself create something worth sharing and following. |
| Stocktwits | Free participation plus paid data depth | Protect the network's ability to grow while testing professional workflow revenue. |
| Seeking Alpha | Editorial contributor marketplace | Commission specific research gaps with clear standards and visible compensation. |

A cleaner interface is helpful, but it does not establish a reason to contribute. Shorted should make an existing research task easier and let participation improve the outcome.

## 2. Shorted's actual starting point

The connected Shorted Admin aggregate snapshot observed at **10 November 2026, 11:42 am AEDT** reports:

| Measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Registered accounts | 139 |
| Accounts that signed in within 30 days | 37 |
| Accounts created within 30 days | 33 |

These come from Firebase account data through Shorted Admin. They measure authentication activity, not website readership, community retention or unique humans. The source reported no warnings. No individual customer data was retrieved for this strategy.

This supports a concentrated founding-cohort experiment. We should select initial topics using actual overlap among participants' company interests. The current snapshot does not contain that overlap.

Shorted's public product already supplies starting material: reported short positions, historical charts, screeners, strategy views and ownership pages. Its press page documents shareable and embeddable charts. These public descriptions are useful foundations; this research did not perform an implementation audit of the features. [S10–S12]

Keep the heatmaps, market maps, Sankey views and treemaps central to discovery. Give a user a way to attach a question or observation to what they are examining. The social layer should add human interpretation to the existing visual research experience.

The current homepage strongly emphasises short selling. To welcome bullish, bearish and undecided investors, test broader community positioning while retaining the Shorted brand:

> **Shorted — the ASX research network.**
>
> Follow companies. Compare evidence. Build your view.

This is a positioning hypothesis. It needs testing with people unfamiliar with the product, including whether the name implies that only short sellers belong.

## 3. Start with a specific audience

The founding audience should be self-directed ASX investors who follow a manageable set of companies and want to understand changes. Recruit a few sector specialists who enjoy explaining documents or business mechanics. Professionals and journalists can add value, but the community should function without their daily participation.

| Audience | Reason to read | Reason to contribute | Potential paid workflow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engaged private investor | Understand changes across a watchlist | Ask questions, compare interpretations and receive feedback | Monitoring rules, history, research journal and exports |
| Hobby trader | Company-specific context, charts and conversation | Share an observation, ask a quick question, develop skills | Convenient alerts and analysis tools, if repeat use justifies payment |
| Analyst or sector specialist | Find evidence and informed questions efficiently | Build a useful public work record and exchange specialist knowledge | Private research spaces, source-linked exports and team workflows |
| Journalist or newsletter author | Find public evidence, unresolved questions and changes | Ask bounded factual questions and credit helpful sources | Reporting packets and collaborative research tools |
| Company or IR representative | Understand questions about published information | Answer factual questions with disclosed affiliation | Organised Q&A and aggregate engagement tools later |

Participation should not require public portfolio values, a legal name or brokerage integration. Professional identity and company affiliation can be optional, separately verified attributes. No badge should imply that a person is correct about an investment.

## 4. The central product: a company research record with conversation

The basic unit should be a **company event or research question**, connected to the person asking it and the evidence used to answer it.

A proposed company page has four useful areas:

1. **Overview:** Shorted's charts, positioning, ownership and dated source context.
2. **Discussion:** current questions, observations and ordinary conversation.
3. **Research:** member notes and theses that can be saved, revised and followed.
4. **Events:** announcements, disclosures and other supported events, with relevant discussions attached.

A newcomer should understand the current questions in minutes. A returning participant should see what changed since their last review.

### Keep contribution easy

The initial composer needs three entry points:

| Contribution | Minimum useful input | Optional depth |
|---|---|---|
| Ask a question | Company or selected chart/event, then one sentence | Relevant source, what the user has checked |
| Share an observation | A short explanation, optionally attached to existing data | Source excerpt, chart annotation, comparison |
| Publish research | A view and its reasoning | Assumptions, time horizon, risks, evidence and what would change the view |

Informal conversation and humour can coexist with substantive research. Request supporting evidence for consequential factual claims; do not require a filing for every reply. Progressive prompts should help users contribute without turning the composer into a form-filling task.

Use response options that communicate meaning: helpful explanation, useful source, needs checking and alternative view. A report of abuse is a different action from disagreement. A simple reaction should not become a claim of verified correctness.

### A hypothetical member journey

An ASX company publishes a capital-raising announcement. Shorted links the event to the original document and the relevant chart.

A member asks whether an ownership percentage changed because of selling or because the number of shares changed. Another member provides the relevant calculation and disclosure. Someone else identifies a remaining uncertainty. The original contributor updates their research note, preserving the earlier version.

Followers receive a concise update when the unanswered question gets new evidence. The result is useful to the original members and to the next person researching the company.

This example describes proposed behaviour; it does not claim that a specific company's event has been verified.

## 5. The social network and its retention loop

Users should be able to follow **companies, people and particular research questions**. Each relationship answers a different need:

- Company follow: tell me about relevant new activity.
- Person follow: show me new work by someone whose thinking I find useful.
- Question follow: tell me when this particular uncertainty develops.

The home experience can combine those relationships, with controls for topic, time horizon and notification frequency. Keep a chronological option and explain why an item is shown. Start ranking with explicit preferences and human curation; an elaborate engagement algorithm is premature.

The intended loop is:

**New evidence → a question or observation → useful peer response → an improved research note → a saved follow-up → return when evidence changes.**

A separate distribution loop lets an author share a dated note or annotated chart, a reader inspect it without registration, and an interested person follow the author or company. Shorted already documents shareable charts, so the opportunity is to extend their usefulness rather than rebuild the sharing layer wholesale. [S12]

Readers can participate in other communities as well. Authors should be able to link to or export their own work and retain attribution. Portable work makes initial participation easier; ongoing relationships and useful updates supply the reason to return.

## 6. Why knowledgeable contributors would participate

The early contributor offer should be concrete:

- Good research tools that save preparation time.
- A thoughtful response to their work.
- A clear public record of explanations, corrections and follow-through.
- Attribution when their contribution is used in a community brief.
- A small peer group with overlapping interests.
- Recognition for helping other members understand something difficult.
- Tool credits or bounded commissioned work where appropriate.

Strawman's credits and Seeking Alpha's coverage incentives show existing approaches, but Shorted needs its own acceptance criteria and an affordable programme. [S6, S9]

Useful commission examples include explaining a disclosure, reconciling a data inconsistency or documenting a company's stated assumptions. Compensation and editorial scope should be visible. Review the actual programme's licensing implications: paying for financial commentary can change the regulatory analysis, and a fixed fee or credit does not automatically resolve it. [S13]

Do not begin with rewards for post counts, bullish reactions, trading frequency or returns over a short interval. Those incentives can reward attention and risk-taking rather than research quality. This is an incentive-design assessment, not a claim that every existing reward system fails.

### Community rituals

For the pilot, use a small, repeatable schedule:

- A weekly source-linked briefing: what changed and which questions remain.
- One hosted company discussion or 30-minute research session.
- A short review of an assumption someone changed and the evidence behind the change.
- A welcoming thread for questions that newer participants are reluctant to ask.

Hosts should introduce people with overlapping interests and make sure useful contributions receive acknowledgement. Product automation cannot substitute for this early community work.

## 7. Reputation and trust

A single “best investor” score would merge different qualities and invite gaming. Present understandable signals separately:

| Signal | What it can legitimately mean |
|---|---|
| Identity or affiliation checked | The stated identity or organisational relationship was checked |
| Topic contribution history | The person has contributed useful work in a defined area |
| Peer acknowledgement | Other members found a particular contribution helpful |
| Corrections and follow-through | The author updated claims and acknowledged material mistakes |
| Position disclosure | A timestamped self-declaration, unless a separately described verification exists |
| Forecast or model history, if introduced later | Outcomes of explicitly recorded predictions under a published methodology |

“Source attached” only means a source is present. It should not be presented as a truth badge. A professional credential should not automatically increase the rank of the person's investment opinion.

For formal research, preserve dated versions, assumptions, material edits and relevant disclosures. Allow ordinary corrections and moderator removal where appropriate; accountability does not require permanently exposing harmful personal information or unlawful material.

If performance statistics are introduced, require declared horizons, benchmark and corporate-action handling, the full eligible history and unsuccessful cases. Separate paper performance from linked holdings. This work is outside the initial community MVP.

Disclosures should be easy to maintain: position category and relevant company, employment, sponsorship or paid-research relationships. Prefill known choices and make changes easy. Exact position sizes are unnecessary for the initial design.

## 8. Moderation is part of the experience

Start with clear rules, a report queue, rate limits for new accounts, mute/block tools, visible correction paths and human escalation. Review contested factual claims separately from ordinary differences of opinion. A bearish argument deserves the same treatment as a bullish one.

The first release should concentrate discussion in public spaces. Unsolicited private messaging adds risk and operating work before it has demonstrated value. ASIC's 2026 warning describes impersonation and private-chat funnels using planted success claims. This supports verifying organisational claims and closely reviewing external-chat solicitation. [S14]

ASIC also describes coordinated schemes that specify a stock, a buying time and a target before selling. Shorted should prohibit coordinated-buy campaigns and give moderators a way to examine repeated or coordinated activity. Enthusiasm alone is not proof of manipulation. [S15]

The contributor and recommendation model needs an Australian financial-services review before paid stock picks, trade-linked referrals or execution features. ASIC INFO 269 explains that the presentation of factual information and payment for commentary can matter. Calling content “research” does not decide its legal classification. [S13]

If companies later buy services, they should pay for workflow capabilities. They should receive no authority over independent criticism, research ranking, moderation or member reputation. Verification of an affiliation should be available independently of a paid promotional package.

## 9. AI's useful role

AI can lower the effort required to participate:

- Attach relevant existing Shorted data to a question.
- Draft a source-linked summary for a human to review.
- Identify duplicate questions and suggest earlier discussions.
- Flag apparent unit, date or calculation conflicts for checking.
- Compare a new filing with the assumptions in a saved note.
- Produce a digest that credits the contributors whose work it summarises.

AI-generated community posts should be explicitly attributable to Shorted or their author. Do not create fake participants, replies, endorsements or enthusiasm.

A question answered with invented certainty is worse than a visible unresolved question. Preserve links to evidence and allow “not established by available sources.” Access permissions must apply to AI retrieval and summaries, including private team material.

## 10. Pilot design

### Founding cohort

Recruit **24 consenting participants**, with a planning target of approximately 16 engaged private investors, six experienced researchers or sector specialists, and two occasional journalist/educator participants. These are recruitment hypotheses, not verified available members. Company representatives can be invited for specific public-document sessions later; their participation is not a launch dependency.

Use **three initial circles**, covering roughly two companies each. Select the companies from actual participant overlap and Shorted's data readiness. A few circles with reliable replies are more testable than broad coverage with sparse conversation.

Each circle needs a named host, at least six interested participants and two people willing to contribute regularly. Participation can overlap. Public stock pages outside the pilot remain useful through their data, with questions directed to the active circles when relevant.

Budget an initial **8–12 hours per week of human hosting, curation and moderation** across the pilot. This is a proposed allocation cap to test, not an estimate validated by operating data; engineering and legal work are separate. Track actual time.

### First 90 days, conditional on feature readiness

| Phase | Work | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Participant interest survey; select circles; appoint hosts; publish norms; instrument events; prepare initial factual briefings and real open questions | At least six interested participants and two willing contributors per circle; working report and correction path |
| Weeks 3–8 | Run the six-week community pilot; publish weekly briefs; host bounded sessions; collect qualitative task outcomes | Repeat use, timely replies, member-led contributions and manageable host effort |
| Weeks 9–12 | Improve weak workflows, test paid research-tool continuation, and expand one circle at a time if gates pass | Evidence of useful peer relationships and paid workflow demand; room-level contribution remains healthy |

This is an experiment schedule, not a committed engineering delivery estimate. The pilot can begin with a small, manually curated experience once the minimum controls exist.

### Metrics and proposed decision gates

The following thresholds are **our initial hypotheses**, not industry benchmarks. At this scale, show counts and individual task examples alongside rates.

| Question | Proposed gate |
|---|---|
| Do invited participants activate? | At least 18 of 24 follow relevant companies/circles and complete a meaningful action within the first two pilot weeks |
| Do members receive help? | At least 70% of a minimum 20 genuine member questions receive a useful response within 48 hours; classify staff and peer responses separately |
| Does activity spread beyond staff? | At least two regular non-staff contributors in each circle, and at least six distinct non-staff contributors across each of the final two pilot weeks |
| Do people return? | At least 12 of the original 24 complete a meaningful action in both the fifth and sixth pilot weeks |
| Is contribution becoming reciprocal? | At least half of substantive contributions in the final two weeks come from unpaid, non-staff participants |
| Can users name the benefit? | At least eight participants identify a specific question resolved, mistake corrected or research task made easier |
| Is operation sustainable? | Actual hosting/moderation effort remains within the agreed cap, or there is a credible funded plan for the extra effort |
| Does a paid proposition exist? | Run a separate explicit paid-continuation test for the workflow; a free community compliment is not a payment signal |

A meaningful action can include reading a research item and opening its source, saving it for a real task, asking/answering a question, or updating research. A sign-in or notification open alone is insufficient. Helpful readers count; everyone does not need to become an author.

Audit the relevance of replies and the usefulness of source references. Counting URLs or reactions would allow the metrics to be gamed.

### Failure and adaptation

If fewer than eight founders return weekly by the end, or staff still supply most useful work, stop opening new circles. Try a smaller research club or a strong watchlist digest with occasional hosted discussions.

If participation is dominated by two people or one promotional faction, recruit for missing perspectives and improve facilitation before expanding. If users enjoy the research but seldom interact, a research subscription may still be viable; social-network demand has not been established.

If attention appears only during sharp price movements, test usefulness during ordinary reporting periods. If impersonation or coordinated promotion repeatedly exceeds moderation capacity, pause the implicated feature or invitations while addressing the failure.

## 11. Monetisation and economics

Keep public reading, basic following, questions and contributions free. Contributors should not need to buy a subscription to help the network.

Potential paid value should sit around work:

| Customer | Candidate paid capability |
|---|---|
| Individual investor | Advanced monitoring, saved research history, custom digests and convenient exports |
| Research team | Shared private workspaces, annotations, evidence packets and team permissions |
| Newsroom or newsletter | Reporting workflows, reusable charts and source-linked publishing outputs |
| IR team, later | Public Q&A organisation and aggregate question/engagement analysis with clear commercial boundaries |
| Developer/researcher | Documented licensed data access, coverage and revision information |

Do not promise a price point from the forum sample. Test whether people return and then whether the workflow supports payment.

For scale only, **500 paying accounts at an assumed A$20/month would produce A$10,000 in gross monthly billings**. This is arithmetic, not a forecast or price recommendation. Tax, payment fees, data licences, infrastructure, support, moderation and contributor costs remain to be deducted. It does not establish profitability.

A small, useful community can therefore be commercially meaningful without matching an incumbent's total traffic. The economics still need observed conversion, retention and service cost.

## 12. What could become defensible

The UI and generic summaries are reproducible. Potential defensibility develops only if the community repeatedly produces:

- Trusted relationships between people covering the same companies.
- A dated history of questions, evidence, corrections and changed assumptions.
- A record of which contributors help with particular research tasks.
- Personal research context that is updated by new events.
- A recognisable editorial and moderation standard.
- Shareable work that earns distribution from people who use it.

This is a proposed path to a defensible product, not a claim that Shorted already possesses these assets. Original member contributions and their histories could matter more than another feed of imported posts. An imported archive would not automatically recreate the relationships that made the original discussions valuable.

The early technical scope should remain small: contributions and revisions, replies, follows, saves, disclosure snapshots, notification preferences and moderation events. Existing data objects should supply context. A broker-linked portfolio layer, elaborate reputation algorithm, creator marketplace and trade execution can wait for distinct validated needs.

## Evidence basis and limits

The earlier 49-thread forum collection supports hypotheses about monitoring, interpretation, data preparation, trust and useful exports. It is a purposive sample; maker posts, indexed excerpts, historical examples and overlapping participants limit generalisation.

This strategy also uses current published competitor documentation and the aggregate Shorted Admin snapshot. It does not infer the quality of an incumbent's enforcement from its written rules, nor treat competitor marketing claims as independent performance evidence. No participants have been contacted, no invitations sent, and no product changes deployed as part of this research.
