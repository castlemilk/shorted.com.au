# Blog thumbnail art direction

The cover should show the article's idea before the reader sees its title. Build a small editorial illustration around a concrete subject and one visual action: a threshold breaks, a queue narrows, a price tag is trimmed, evidence is uncovered. The collection should feel like one publication with different stories, rather than one financial chart with different titles.

The reusable workflow is [Shorted editorial art](../.claude/skills/shorted-editorial-art/SKILL.md), also linked from the [newsroom skill](../.claude/skills/newsroom/SKILL.md). Use it for both new images and revisions.

## The Shorted cover construct

Bold tactile editorial illustration connects the collection. Build each cover from **one idea, one decisive visual moment, one dominant silhouette and deliberate colour contrast**. The subject comes from the article; the composition makes its relationship or tension visible. Texture finishes the image after that structure works.

| Brief element | Decision to make | Approved example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject and action | What concrete thing explains the story, and what is it doing? | A contour sheet curls away from a street print to reveal another data layer. |
| Compositional hook | What draws the eye immediately? | An oversized diagonal fold, unequal reel sizes or a visible snapped gap. |
| Large shapes | Which silhouette dominates, and what separates it from its ground? | An ivory house and dark bracing stand against oxblood. |
| Colour field | Which restrained field helps this particular subject? | Ink green for layered suburb research; mineral blue for cable and steel. |
| Material treatment | Which consistent making process suits the idea? | Cut paper, spare woodcut or a physically coherent sculptural still life. |
| Caveat | What must the image avoid implying? | The house remains intact; failed short bets do not mean housing cannot fall. |

Use about two thirds of the frame for the subject and action as a starting point. A close crop can lose incidental edges but must keep important joints, connections and the decisive moment visible. Vary viewpoint, light/dark balance and medium across the collection. Warm ivory, charcoal and selective amber supply continuity; stronger subject-specific fields supply variety.

Acceptance requires four passes at full size and at 160 × 90: recognisable subject/action, a clear focal moment, credible construction and an honest article-specific claim. Inspect the whole contact sheet before selecting a batch; avoid long runs of the same background, prop, camera angle or texture. These principles apply to future blog and newsroom cover generation as well as the existing collection.

## Composition and style

- Compose for **16:9**, with a strong silhouette that survives at 320 × 180 and 160 × 90. Keep the subject and its action inside the central 80% so alternate crops remain useful.
- Use one dominant subject or a tightly grouped comparison. Give it enough scale to read immediately. Avoid tiny tickers, background charts, fine print and decorative dashboard fragments.
- Keep text, headlines, tickers, percentages, dates and logos out of the generated art. Article titles already appear beside thumbnails. Typeset any necessary social-card copy separately.
- Share warm ink, paper, tactile material and selective amber emphasis with [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md). Let the subject introduce its own restrained palette: oil black, limestone, eucalyptus olive, oxidised metal or brick. Alternate light and dark compositions where the story calls for it.
- Prefer thoughtful cut-paper collage, printmaking, sculptural still life or a close material photograph. Physical depth is useful; toy-like isometric asset packs, glowing fintech wallpaper and repeated fiery effects are not.
- Show an action tied to the article, rather than a generic asset class. A housing article needs its particular measurement or tension; a finance article needs its mechanism or claim.
- Use Australian building forms, industrial subjects or landscape details only where they help identify the story. Do not add an Australia silhouette, gum leaves or a skyline as a universal ASX badge.
- A conceptual visual is not measured data. Keep exact quantities and real comparisons in the article's accessible charts. Do not make a generation look like a verified price chart, trade signal or screenshot of a feature that does not exist.

## Visual impact

Each cover gets one story-specific compositional hook: a surprising scale difference, a revealing fold, a narrow exit, a taut diagonal or a decisive break. Enlarge that action and build clear light/dark shapes around it. Use asymmetric balance, a close crop, a cast shadow or a stronger colour field with a quiet pocket at the focal point. Ink green, mineral blue and oxblood can join the palette when the subject calls for them; vary the set's tonal balance. Keep surfaces and connections believable.

Review at 160 × 90 before judging finish. The focal moment should attract the eye first and remain visibly related to the main subject. Correct similar midtones, excessive empty background, a remote thin action or a weak silhouette through contrast, scale and framing. Adding ornament, glow, explosions or more distressed texture does not create a stronger editorial idea. Preserve the article's uncertainty and avoid exaggerating its claim.

## Craft review

The collection should feel deliberately composed. Prefer a few large, specific forms and honest negative space. Paper edges, ink, wood, steel and glass need their own material behaviour; avoid applying the same distressed texture everywhere. Check supports, joints, continuous ropes, perspective, refraction and shadows. Reject fused parts, inconsistent repeated forms, fake writing, melted detail and unnecessary miniature scenery. Conceptual scale is useful when the action remains clear.

Compare neighbours before accepting a cover. A magnifier, amber sun, torn horizon, gum leaf or position token is not a mandatory brand element. Repetition needs an editorial reason. Do not try to make an image feel crafted by adding grain or more objects. Keep generation provenance and AI credits; this quality standard does not turn generated art into a verified photograph or handmade original.

## Prompt brief and review

Write each prompt from the article's actual argument, not its SEO title alone:

> Create a 16:9 editorial thumbnail for an article about **[specific finding or mechanism]**. Show **[concrete subject]** performing **[one action]**, with **[compositional hook]** drawing the eye to that moment. Build **[large light/dark shapes]** against **[colour field]**. Use **[chosen tactile treatment]**, warm ink/paper and selective amber. Let the subject and action fill roughly two thirds of the frame while keeping essential attachments inside the central 80%. Make the silhouette and action legible at 160 × 90. No words, numbers, logos, decorative charts or generic finance symbols. This is a conceptual illustration, not a data figure. Preserve **[article-specific caveat]**.

Review the result at thumbnail size beside at least two existing covers. A reader should be able to name the subject and action without zooming. Reject an image that repeats another article's motif without a new idea, substitutes atmosphere for meaning, or communicates a stronger claim than the article makes. Save promising alternatives as versioned files and keep the prompt with the asset.

## Publishing workflow

1. Read the article and choose its specific subject, action and caveat from the table above. Follow [article-design.md](article-design.md) for the surrounding layout.
2. Generate through the built-in image tool when working interactively. Review the original and the 160 × 90 crop; retain the exact prompt, mode and provenance. For an existing article, save a new version rather than replacing the old artwork in place. Provenance lives in `output/article-covers/<slug>.png` and `.md` for v1, and `<slug>-vN.png` and `.md` for later versions.
3. Export a 1600 × 900 WebP to `web/public/assets/blog/<slug>/cover-editorial-vN.webp`. Set both `coverImage` and `ogImage.url` to that path and write descriptive `coverAlt`. Keep titles and branding outside the image. From `web/`, run `node --import tsx scripts/editorial-thumbnails.mts --slug=<slug>` (or `--all`) to export the matching 800 × 450 card image and set `thumbnailImage`. Run `--all` after selections to refresh both review sheets and the manifest's selected cover, alternative, thumbnail, prompt and original; missing provenance fails the export. Mastheads, featured stories and social previews retain the full cover.
4. Verify the compiled article and grid card at desktop/mobile widths in both themes. Run `cd web && node --import tsx --test scripts/article-art.test.mts` for real cover/thumbnail dimensions, paths, alternatives and shared generation policy.

`web/scripts/blog-cover.tsx` is now an optional typeset social-card helper. It writes `social-card-v1.png`, refuses to overwrite an existing output and never changes the article's cover. It does not define the illustration style.

The existing automated presets in `scripts/image-gen` and the newsroom art director, fallback prompts and validator in `scripts/take-writer` follow the same subject/action brief, tactile treatments, story-specific colour and wide thumbnail composition. They retain useful bans on baked text, clichés and fabricated evidence while allowing housing architecture and light compositions. Updating these presets does not regenerate stored newsroom images or deploy the pipeline. Future generated imagery must be captioned as illustration, never as a verified photograph of a real event or site.
