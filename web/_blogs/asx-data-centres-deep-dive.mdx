# ASX data centre stocks: the boom, the grid and the shorts

The Reserve Bank's November [Statement on Monetary Policy](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/smp/2026/nov/outlook.html), published on 3 November 2026, forecasts business investment to grow 12.0% over the year to December 2026, faster than any other part of domestic demand. The outlook "has been revised up significantly, largely driven by data centre-related investment", and the RBA expects "around 70 per cent" of the extra spending to be imported. ASIC's report for that day had 11.59% of [NEXTDC](/shorts/NXT)'s shares on issue reported short, its highest reading since January 2020. On 5 November a vehicle owned equally by [Goodman Group](/shorts/GMG) and Digital Realty bought 59,951,306 NEXTDC shares at A$9.50, part of a 9.9% holding built from 3 to 6 November, and NEXTDC closed 50.8% higher.

This piece sets those numbers against the grid, the spending, the power policies and six ASX stocks. It is not a recommendation.

Short positions are ASIC's daily aggregate figures. The newest position date is 3 November 2026, published on 9 November (T+4); the 4 and 5 November reports are due on 10 and 11 November. Prices and volumes are from Shorted's data: price-only closes to 9 November. Percentage changes and the buyer's average price are Shorted's calculations.

## Demand against the grid

AEMO's [2026 Electricity Statement of Opportunities](https://aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/planning_and_forecasting/nem_esoo/2026/2026-electricity-statement-of-opportunities.pdf), published in August, covers the National Electricity Market's five regions: Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia and Tasmania.

Existing sites have headroom: in the March quarter 2026 they used about 27% of their 2.2 GW of maximum connection capacity, by AEMO's estimate. New connections are where the build slows. Proposed connection capacity rose from 38 GW to 67 GW in AEMO's 2026 assessment, yet more than 40% of data-centre projects since 2025 have dropped out or regressed in connection status.

The IEA's [Key Questions on Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) (2026) puts global data-centre electricity use at 485 TWh in 2025 and about 950 TWh by 2030. Its [2025 Energy and AI report](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary) estimated that around 20% of planned data-centre projects could be at risk of delays unless grid risks are addressed.

## Where the money lands

The ABS has no separate data-centre series ([ABS, 26 February 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/spotlight-data-centres-economic-statistics)), but its releases name data centres:

- **Equipment.** Information media and telecommunications equipment spending rose 199.6% in the March quarter 2026 (revised) on "record investment in server racks and processing equipment for data centres", then fell 53.0% in the June quarter ([ABS, 27 August 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/new-capital-expenditure-down-36-cent-june)).
- **Construction.** The same release says "Investment in data centre construction and expansion projects continues to grow, rising for an eighth straight quarter."
- **Approvals.** Non-residential approvals rose 41.0% to a record A$10.83 billion in May 2026, "driven by a rise in large data centre approvals located in New South Wales and Victoria" ([ABS, 1 July 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/dwelling-approvals-fall-may)).

The RBA's Sarah Hunter gave the reason [on 9 November](https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-ag-2026-11-09.html): "Much of the spending is on imported machinery and equipment, particularly IT equipment manufactured overseas, which dampens the net contribution to GDP." The Statement says most of the pipeline is in NSW and Victoria; Shorted's [NSW](/economy/nsw) and [Victoria](/economy/vic) pages show each state's economy beside the ASX companies there ([how the explorer works](/blog/shorted-economy-explorer)).

Amazon announced "a new total of AU$20 billion from 2025 to 2029" for its Australian data centres in [June 2025](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/amazon-data-center-investment-in-australia), and Microsoft A$25 billion "in new digital infrastructure" by the end of 2029 on [23 April 2026](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/investing-in-australias-ai-future/). OpenAI signed a memorandum of understanding with NEXTDC on [5 December 2025](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03037632) for an AI campus at S7 in Eastern Creek; OpenAI "intends to be an initial offtaker", and the first phase is expected in the second half of 2027, subject to approvals. The release gives no capacity or cost.

## Bring your own power

Federal and state governments have moved to make large data centres supply or pay for their own power:

- **23 March, federal.** The [expectations for data centres](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers) say new sites "should not place upward pressure on energy prices", and energy-intensive proposals that do not align "will not be prioritised by Commonwealth regulatory assessments".
- **5 August, energy ministers.** [AEMC advice](https://www.aemc.gov.au/news-centre/media-releases/data-centres-bring-clean-firm-energy-be-flexible-and-pay-their-way-aemc-advises-ministers), considered by ministers on 28 July, recommends four changes: surrender renewable certificates from new generators, show demand is backed by new firm capacity, register as market participants, and connect flexibly.
- **17 August, NSW.** The [policy framework](https://www.infrastructure.nsw.gov.au/news/nsw-releases-data-centre-policy-framework-and-guidelines/) offers assessment within 75 days to projects that meet its measures, and counts 19 data-centre projects, valued at A$50.3 billion, in its State Significant Development pipeline.
- **26 August, National Cabinet.** The [communique](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/meeting-national-cabinet-26-august-26) says governments will develop mandatory standards for data-centre energy, water and land use, with legislation intended in early 2027. A requirement to underwrite new renewable generation was left out after disagreement with Queensland and the Northern Territory, [W.Media reported](https://w.media/australia-agrees-national-data-centre-framework-but-energy-rules-remain-open/).
- **22 September, Victoria.** The [Premier's plan](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/we-need-data-centres-we-set-rules) requires new data centres to "Power themselves – with their own renewable energy supply" and sets a 150-metre buffer from homes. Victoria votes on 28 November.

## NEXTDC: strategic money against short money

[The newsroom's take of 21 October](/news/nxt-shorts-8pc-into-40pc-fall-jemena-m3-delay) covers the guidance cut and the position before it. Jemena suspended parts of the 66kV works needed for the next increase in grid supply to NEXTDC's M3 Melbourne campus and does not expect to restart before February 2027, so that increase moved to FY28 ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03154955)). FY27 underlying EBITDA guidance fell to A$245–265 million from A$385–410 million, and the shares closed at A$5.35 on 21 October, 41.7% below the A$9.18 close of 16 October, the last before the halt.

Reported short positions rose from 8.31% (63,154,467 shares) on 21 October to 10.74% on 29 October, the first reading above 10% since February 2020 ([the alerts post](/blog/shorted-short-interest-alerts) uses that crossing as its example), then to 11.14% on 30 October and 11.59% on 3 November: 88,046,352 of 759,752,814 shares.

Ackland, the Goodman and Digital Realty vehicle, bought 1.05 million shares on 3 November and 6.65 million on 4 November, ran an after-market book build at A$9.50 that evening, and took 59,951,306 shares at that price on 5 November. Its [holder notice](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163543) lists 217 purchases totalling 75,215,528 shares, or 9.9%. Summed, they cost A$693.7 million, an average of A$9.22, the figure in Goodman and Digital Realty's [statement](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163466) of 9 November. Neither "has any present intention of submitting a control proposal, or making a takeover offer for NEXTDC", it said, though both reserve the right. NEXTDC's board called it "an opportunistic move" on 5 November and [said on 9 November](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163616) it had received no proposal. The shares closed at A$6.53 on 4 November, A$9.85 on 5 November, when more than 81 million traded, and A$10.52 on 9 November.

What short sellers did on 5 November is not yet published; ASIC's reports will show only the net change. One documented source of hedging: NEXTDC's A$1.1 billion convertible notes, priced on 10 September, came with a delta placement of about 18.6 million borrowed shares for "the initial delta hedge required by investors in the Convertible Notes" ([ASX release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260910/pdf/073y1w4rflr2ry.pdf)). How much of that hedging remains in ASIC's total is not disclosed.

## Rates meet the capex cycle

The RBA's cash rate target has been 4.85% since 4 November, the fifth increase of 2026 from 3.60% ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/cash-rate/)). Three readings:

- **CDC**, 49.69% owned by [Infratil](/shorts/IFT), was valued "broadly flat" at A$18.45 billion at 30 September after "a material increase in the forward yield curve"; its cost of equity rose to 12.58%, and its net debt by A$1,161 million in the quarter ([Infratil, 7 October](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03149192)).
- **NEXTDC** guides FY27 capital expenditure of A$4.5–5.0 billion, including about A$230 million of capitalised interest.
- **[DigiCo Infrastructure REIT](/shorts/DGT)** traded at 0.59 times book value at the 9 November close, on Shorted's fundamentals.

The [rate-rise analysis](/blog/asx-rate-hikes-banks-landlords-retailers) covers lenders and landlords.

## The contractors

Contractors carry the construction work:

- **[Southern Cross Electrical Engineering](/shorts/SXE)** turned over A$120 million of data-centre work in FY26 and is "forecasting circa $400m in FY27" ([AGM presentation, 27 October](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03157360)); its [FY26 results](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03126581) name NEXTDC's S4 and DigiCo's SYD1 among its projects.
- **[Downer](/shorts/DOW)** cites an "$11.8bn Data Centre infrastructure opportunity pipeline" ([FY26 results](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03127531)).
- **[Ventia](/shorts/VNT)** fell 16.2% on 9 November, from A$5.73 to A$4.80, after saying "trading conditions in Telecommunications and Energy continue to be challenging, reflecting the deferral of expected work volumes" ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163278)). The release does not mention data centres.

### NEXTDC (NXT)

- **What the data shows:** 11.59% short on 3 November, against 5.89% on 3 November 2025; A$10.52 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** the guidance cut and Ackland's 9.9%, above.
- **The case others make for it:** NEXTDC reports a pro forma forward order book of 565.1MW of binding contracts not yet billing at 30 June ([FY26 results](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260827/pdf/073c04gt4ts40y.pdf)) and A$8,675 million of liquidity at 30 September.
- **The case others make against it:** NEXTDC warns that service level credits on the M3 contracts "could reduce Underlying EBITDA below the revised guidance".
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 20 November, by which the capex review outcome is due, with a conditional board spill resolution ([notice of meeting](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03153944)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether short sellers sold into the book build or covered on 5 November.

### Goodman Group (GMG)

- **What the data shows:** 1.66% short on 3 November, against 0.34% a year earlier; A$28.17 on 9 November, after a 7.0% rise on 5 November.
- **What happened:** its [Q1 FY27 operational update](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03161755) on 4 November, and its 50% share of Ackland.
- **The case others make for it:** in that update, data centres under construction are 79% of Goodman's A$19.7 billion of work in progress, with a 6.6 GW power bank, 3.6 GW of it secured.
- **The case others make against it:** most of that work began before customer contracts were signed, Goodman says, so work in progress is 31% pre-committed.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 19 November ([Goodman](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03140019)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether the shares short are outright positions or hedges.

### Infratil (IFT)

- **What the data shows:** 0.97% short on 3 November, against all 1.0 billion shares (the primary listing is the NZX, and the accounts are in New Zealand dollars); A$10.58 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** on 21 October Infratil said CDC's "power delivery and construction timelines are tracking as anticipated" ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03155155)).
- **The case others make for it:** CDC's FY27 EBITDAF guidance rose to A$710–750 million on 16 September ([Infratil](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03139851)).
- **The case others make against it:** the flat valuation and the rise in net debt, above.
- **Next dated event:** interim results on 18 November ([Infratil](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03152198)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** short sales made only on the NZX.

### DigiCo Infrastructure REIT (DGT)

- **What the data shows:** 4.79% short on 3 November, against 7.92% a year earlier (26.8 million shares, from 43.7 million); A$2.45 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** a 15-year, 71MW contract on 15 October left SYD1 100% contracted across 88MW ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03152532)).
- **The case others make for it:** the customer's investment grade credit support, and reaffirmed FY27 underlying EBITDA guidance of A$120–125 million.
- **The case others make against it:** the first 10MW of a 52MW expansion is expected to produce income from late Q4 FY27, and the units trade below book value.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 11 November ([DigiCo](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03141250)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** who closed those positions, or why.

### Macquarie Technology Group (MAQ)

- **What the data shows:** 3.41% short on 3 November, against 2.50% a year earlier; A$50.05 on 9 November, 24.7% below its 31 December close.
- **What happened:** its FY26 results on 26 August ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03130713)).
- **The case others make for it:** twelve consecutive years of EBITDA growth, to A$115.9 million, and A$200 million from the National Reconstruction Fund Corporation.
- **The case others make against it:** FY27 EBITDA is guided to "modest growth", and initial construction of a planned campus of about 200MW is estimated to finish in late 2029.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 25 November ([Macquarie Technology](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03156274)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** much on its own: MAQ has 25.8 million shares on issue, so the year's 0.91-point rise is 235,028 shares.

### Southern Cross Electrical Engineering (SXE)

- **What the data shows:** 3.06% short on 3 November, from 0.04% on 3 August with shares on issue almost unchanged; A$3.61 on 9 November, 22.0% below its 9 October close.
- **What happened:** on 12 October it announced a deal to buy Entire, a Victorian fire-protection and HVAC services group, for up to A$112.1 million and lifted FY27 EBITDA guidance to at least A$110 million ([ASX release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03150892)); its AGM presentation followed on 27 October, and the purchase completed on 6 November.
- **The case others make for it:** an "Unprecedented pipeline of data centre projects regardless of recent high-profile corporate events", and a record A$810 million order book at 30 June.
- **The case others make against it:** NEXTDC, a named customer, has "paused projects not backed by customer contracts".
- **Next dated event:** none set by SXE in the documents checked; NEXTDC's capex review is due by 20 November.
- **What this cannot tell you:** why the position built after August.

## What the data cannot tell you

ASIC's reports name no short sellers. A position in the file may be a hedge (NEXTDC's convertible notes are one documented source), one leg of a pair trade or an outright position, and the file does not say which. AEMO covers the NEM only, the ABS counts data centres inside broader categories, and the RBA's numbers are forecasts.
