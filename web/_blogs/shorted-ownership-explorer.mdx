# Who Holds ASX Stocks

Shorted has always answered one question about an ASX company: how much of it is sold short. The ownership explorer answers the next one: who holds it.

It is built from records that are already public. A holder must lodge a substantial holding notice when its interest reaches 5% of a company's votes, when that interest moves by a percentage point or more, and when it falls below 5%. Index funds publish their holdings files, some of them daily. Super funds publish their portfolio holdings twice a year. Each figure on the site carries the date of the document it came from, and a document Shorted cannot read reliably is left out rather than guessed.

It is free, at **[shorted.com.au/ownership](/ownership)**.

Each figure is the notice's own, as at its date of change, and links to the notice on ASX. The register draws the same record as Holder Lanes: one line per holder, on its own scale from the 5% threshold up, stepping at each notice read, with a dotted tail for the time since the last one. A holder that ceases drops back to the 5% line, marked "under 5%", because a notice of ceasing states no figure below it. State Street's lane on PLS steps four times in a fortnight: 11.29% for 23 October, 12.37% for 28 October, 13.8% for 30 October and 12.66% for 3 November.

The page's header also prints ASIC's short position, 16.81% for 2 November. Above the lanes, its readout adds two figures from the fund files, each with its own date: the share of PLS held by the index funds Shorted tracks (3.53%, from 16 of the 35 funds, in files dated 30 September to 5 November 2026) and the combined value held by the super fund options it tracks (all 11 hold PLS, $579.5 million at 30 June 2026).

### Why the figures do not add up

The nine figures add to 79.88%. The register prints 62.10%, because Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group's notices include the interests of First Sentier, which it owns, and of Morgan Stanley, in which it holds more than 20% of the votes. Shorted counts the three once, at the largest of their figures. Even 62.10% is not the share of PLS these holders own:

- **The dates differ.** The notices were released between 26 June 2025 and 5 November 2026, and each figure stands until the same holder's next notice replaces it. The register marks any figure older than 90 days with its age.
- **A relevant interest is wider than ownership.** Banks' and brokers' notices are often securities lending, and a lent share can appear in the lender's notice and the borrower's at once, or in the lender's notice and on the share register under a buyer's name. [Our guide to substantial holder notices](/blog/substantial-holder-notices-explained) walks through the rules.
- **Below 5% there are no notices.** Most of a company's holders never appear.

So every sum on the site is labelled for what it is: what the notices disclose, with each interest counted once where an overlap is known.

## Then the whole market

The [Overview](/ownership) and six instrument pages read the same record across the market:

- **[Market map](/ownership/map):** ASX sectors and companies as a treemap, sized by market cap, disclosed exposure or index fund holdings, and coloured by what the notices disclose, investors' interests, hedge funds, index funds, short interest or a change over the chosen window.
- **[Connections](/ownership/connections):** how each kind of holder, from super funds and index managers to banks and brokers, connects to each sector, by estimated exposure (the voting power a notice states, times the company's market cap).
- **[Activity](/ownership/activity):** notices by sector and week over the last year, each week's notices one click away.
- **[Funds](/ownership/funds):** each tracked index fund's and super option's ASX holdings and sector weights, what changed between two of an index fund's files, and two funds side by side.
- **[Holders](/ownership/holders):** the institutions, largest first, with each one's disclosed connections by sector and company.
- **[Changes](/ownership/changes):** two snapshots of an index fund compared, beside the notices released in between.

Select anything drawn, such as a company on the map, a band, a week or a fund holding, and a panel beside it (a sheet on smaller screens) explains the selection. It starts with one sentence built from the figures it shows, never written by a model. The selection is part of the page's address, so a shared link opens on the same company, band or week.

Two lists sit behind the instruments. [Notices](/ownership/notices) has every notice read, newest release first, filtered by window, event, kind of holder, company or holder. [Registers](/ownership/stocks) has every company with a current substantial holder, sortable eleven ways, from what its notices disclose to its short position. Each of the 146 institutions on Shorted's map has a page of its own, such as [State Street's](/ownership/state-street) or [AustralianSuper's](/ownership/australiansuper), with its current substantial holdings, its notices and its funds' largest holdings. On a stock page, a Substantial holders card on the Company tab and a one-line strip on the Overview link to the full register.

## How to read it

- **Current means the newest notice read.** A holding stands until the same holder's next notice replaces it. A notice of ceasing that could not be read leaves the older figure in place, so every row carries its date, and the register flags any figure older than 90 days.
- **Banks' and brokers' notices are often securities lending.** They are shown beside investors and never counted as investors. A "crowded" register counts super funds, index managers, active managers, hedge funds, sovereign funds and insurers only.
- **A fall is a fall in the notice's figure.** A holder's percentage also falls when a company issues new shares, so the site never calls a lower figure a disposal.
- **A super disclosure is one investment option**, not the whole fund.
- **Short interest is ASIC's**, published four trading days after the date it describes.

## Decisions behind it

**Withhold rather than guess.** A document is read as a notice only when its holder, its company and its figures can be read and agree with one another. The Overview reports everything else by outcome. To 5 November, 5,544 notices were withheld:

Another 1,134 documents were set aside: 837 not yet downloaded, which are retried, 277 that were not substantial holding forms and 20 that ASX no longer serves.

**A second reader, with checks.** A notice the parser withholds because it is a scan, states no voting power it can read or names no holder is now read again by language models. One reads it, a second reads it independently, and a third settles a disagreement. A reading is stored only when the readers agree, the form matches the ASX headline, a change notice states both its previous and present figures, the holder is named in the document, and the votes imply a share count within a tenth of ASIC's at the time. Anything else stays withheld. So far 1,896 notices have been read this way, among them BlackRock's 6.54% on PLS, and each carries a "model-read" mark in the notice lists.

**One holder's history at a time.** The Holder Lanes step each holder from notice to notice. Shorted does not draw a company's whole register over time: rebuilding past registers from a record with withheld notices and nothing below 5% would be guessing.

**Every figure carries its own date.** Notices are read to 5 November 2026, index fund files are dated 30 September to 5 November, super options are as at 30 June, and ASIC's short positions are for 2 November. No page prints one "as at" for all of them.

<Callout type="warning" title="A holding is not a view">

A substantial holding notice discloses who can vote or dispose of shares, as at a date. It is not a position for or against the company, and an index fund holds a company because the company is in its index. Nothing here is a recommendation.
