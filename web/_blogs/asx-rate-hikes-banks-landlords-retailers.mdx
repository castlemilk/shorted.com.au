# RBA rate hikes: ASX shorts build in lenders and landlords

On 3 November 2026, the latest position date in ASIC's data, 288.73 million shares in [CBA](/shorts/CBA), [Westpac](/shorts/WBC), [NAB](/shorts/NAB) and [ANZ](/shorts/ANZ) were reported short, more than the 277.33 million of 10 May 2016. The four have been above that count in every daily report since 26 October. Westpac's 3.19% of shares on issue is above its 2.99% of 10 May 2016. Shorted's equal-weight average for banks was 2.67% in October, against 1.06% in November 2025; for discretionary retailers it was 1.57%, against 1.51%.

Five rate rises into 2026, short positions have built in the banks that lend to households, the landlords of the centres where they shop and the companies that depend on people moving house, but barely across retail as a whole. Households are squeezed, yet spending is still growing and arrears are close to flat.

Positions are from ASIC's daily aggregate reports, as a percentage of shares on issue. The 3 November file was published on 9 November, four trading days later (T+4), and the 4 November file is due on 10 November. Year-earlier positions are for 4 November 2025, and changes are in percentage points (pp). Share prices are price-only closes to 9 November, from Shorted's data. Earlier bank records are in [Shorting Australian bank stocks](/blog/shorting-australian-bank-stocks) and the bond-market channels in the newsroom's [US bond rout piece](/news/us-bond-rout-australia-banks-property-shorts).

## The squeeze, in numbers

On 3 November the Reserve Bank raised the cash rate target 25 basis points to 4.85%, effective the next day, by six votes to three ([RBA statement](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-30.html)). It was the fifth rise of 2026, 125 basis points from 3.60%, and the highest target since early December 2008 ([cash rate table](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/cash-rate/)). The statement says "housing prices have fallen significantly".

- **Prices.** The monthly CPI rose 4.3% over the year to September, with automotive fuel up 23.6% and electricity 12.8% ([ABS, 28 October](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/consumer-price-index-australia/sep-2026)).
- **Real wages.** The Wage Price Index rose 3.2% over the year to the June quarter ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/wage-price-index-australia/jun-2026)), against 3.9% for the quarterly CPI. The RBA's real-wage measure, the index deflated by the CPI, fell 0.8% over that year and is forecast to fall 1.0% over the year to December ([Statement on Monetary Policy, Table 3.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/smp/2026/nov/outlook.html)).
- **Confidence.** The Westpac-Melbourne Institute index fell 4.7% to 80.4 in October; those surveyed after the September rise was announced read 67.2, a level the [Westpac bulletin](https://library.westpaciq.com.au/content/dam/public/westpaciq/secure/economics/documents/aus/2026/10/er20261006BullConsumerSentiment.pdf) says complete surveys have registered only "during the depths of the early 1990s recession". November's reading is due at 11 am AEDT on 10 November, after this was written.
- **Housing.** Cotality's national index fell 1.2% in October to 6.7% below its March peak, and it estimates sales over the past three months were 23% lower than a year earlier ([Cotality](https://www.cotality.com/au/insights/articles/housing-downturn-broadens-as-brisbane-and-perth-lead-the-decline)).

Spending has not cracked. The ABS household spending indicator was 6.8% higher than a year earlier in September, in current prices ([ABS, 4 November](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/finance/monthly-household-spending-indicator/sep-2026)). That nominal figure cannot be set against the 4.3% CPI as if the gap were real growth; the ABS volume measure rose 3.2% over the year to the September quarter. Households saved 6.5% of disposable income in the June quarter ([national accounts](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/national-accounts/australian-national-accounts-national-income-expenditure-and-product/jun-2026)). The RBA found "no evidence of a material slowing in consumption growth in recent months", while warning that very weak sentiment "could be pointing to a more pronounced slowing".

## Lenders: record positions, low arrears

A year earlier the majors were at 0.64% (CBA), 0.60% (Westpac), 0.40% (NAB) and 0.82% (ANZ). [Bendigo and Adelaide Bank](/shorts/BEN) and [Bank of Queensland](/shorts/BOQ) were lower than on 2 October. [Judo Capital](/shorts/JDO), a lender to small and medium businesses, rose from 2.68% to 4.40% in the month, above the 3.60% of 26 June that the October bank piece recorded as its series high.

NAB's $403 million is a judgement about the outlook rather than losses already made, and most of its $1,083 million of individually assessed charges came from business lending. ANZ said on 9 November that Australian retail customers were "largely resilient" but that "for some customers, financial pressure did become more evident in the second half" ([ANZ](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261109/pdf/076cdpj3dr3t8s.pdf)). The RBA's estimate predates the September and November rises.

The bank share count has more than quadrupled in a year while arrears are close to flat, and the file cannot say which reading is right. A bank short can be a view that credit costs and house prices are about to turn, or a hedge of a bank hybrid (a capital note that can convert into shares) or of an index position in a bank-heavy S&P/ASX 200, or one leg of a basis or pair trade. Holding one over an ex-dividend date costs the dividend: Westpac went ex 77c on 5 November, NAB goes ex 85c on 10 November and ANZ ex 83c on 12 November ([Westpac](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261102/pdf/0763k5lj8hxnfd.pdf), [NAB](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261105/pdf/0767n3psp27wgw.pdf), [ANZ](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261109/pdf/076cdmcqsbps8k.pdf)).

## Landlords and a department store

[Vicinity Centres](/shorts/VCX) built through October, from 3.87% on 2 October to 5.04% on 30 October and 5.01% on 3 November.

David Jones went into voluntary administration on 5 November ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-05/david-jones-collapses-into-voluntary-administration/107347878)), two days after the latest position date. Two listed landlords are exposed differently:

- **Vicinity** has four David Jones stores, about $34 million or 2.4% of gross rental income, with $2.2 million unpaid at 31 October and no bank guarantees. It said the effect on FY27 guidance "is currently uncertain and remains under review" ([Vicinity, 5 November](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261105/pdf/0768db869k1zcn.pdf)).
- **[Scentre Group](/shorts/SCG)** has David Jones in 17 Westfield centres, about 1.8% of gross rent, and holds about $30 million of bank guarantees, eight months' rent; rent was paid to 30 September ([Scentre, 4 November](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261104/pdf/0766qkhhl6nkyr.pdf)). It was 0.59% short.

Vicinity's total sales rose 3.0% in the September quarter while department stores fell 6.5% ([AGM update](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261028/pdf/075wlnfvsh0lzf.pdf)).

## Housing turnover: conveyancing and furniture

- **[PEXA](/shorts/PXA)** settles about 90% of Australia's property transfers. Its 28 August guidance expects the economy "to dampen property transaction volumes" ([FY26 results](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260828/pdf/073cr30kn9sfbq.pdf)), and interim CFO Liz Warrell said on the results call that PEXA expects "a double-digit fall in transfer transaction volumes in FY 2027", after July transfers fell 15% ([transcript, Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-pexa-falls-18-as-fy-2027-outlook-turns-cautious-93CH-4880429)).
- **[Nick Scali](/shorts/NCK)** said on 21 October that local written sales orders were 15% lower in the first quarter and store traffic about 10% lower ([trading update](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261021/pdf/075m084j9qv554.pdf)). Its position rose from 6.54% to 7.33% the next day.
- **[Harvey Norman](/shorts/HVN)** was 3.35% short and **[Temple & Webster](/shorts/TPW)** 5.06%, against 1.19% and 0.89% a year earlier. Temple & Webster's buy-back cut its shares on issue from 120.3 million to 116.5 million, but its shares short rose from 1.1 million to 5.9 million.

Over the year to 9 November, on prices alone, Temple & Webster fell 72.6%, PEXA 52.1%, Nick Scali 42.8% and Harvey Norman 40.1%, while the SPDR S&P/ASX 200 fund (STW) rose 2.4%.

The aggregate data is softer. ABS spending on furnishings and household equipment was 3.5% higher than a year earlier in September, in current prices, and fell month on month in August and September; in volume terms it was still 4.2% higher over the year to the September quarter. The fall shows first in turnover.

## Consumer credit: Zip's US exposure

[Zip Co](/shorts/ZIP) was 13.95% short on 3 November, against 2.17% a year earlier. A buy-back cut its shares on issue from 1,287.8 million to 1,245.9 million, but its shares short rose from 27.9 million to 173.8 million, so this is new shorting. The position hit 15.84% on 23 October, the day Zip's first-quarter update reported $10.1 million of costs from "an external fraud event in the US" ([Zip](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261023/pdf/075nxpy4m6tsqs.pdf)); by 3 November the count was 23.6 million shares lower. US customers made A$3.63 billion, or 79%, of the quarter's A$4.60 billion of transaction volume, so a Zip short is mostly exposure to American consumers and to Zip's controls. Local consumer lenders are barely shorted: [Humm](/shorts/HUM) 0.92%, [Credit Corp](/shorts/CCP) 1.70% and [MoneyMe](/shorts/MME) 0.0002%.

## Who covered

In the month to 3 November, with shares on issue all but unchanged, positions fell at [Breville](/shorts/BRG) (8.25% to 7.05%), PEXA (6.64% to 5.79%), Bendigo (5.76% to 5.02%), Nick Scali (6.98% to 6.72%) and BOQ (4.25% to 3.99%). Four-fifths of Breville's FY26 product revenue came from outside Asia-Pacific ([FY26 report](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260819/pdf/072x5fpvmsbz8q.pdf)), so it is a weak guide to Australian households.

Over the year the larger exits were in consumer services, where Shorted's average fell from 3.54% to 2.06%: [Domino's](/shorts/DMP) went from 17.54% to 10.09% and [Guzman y Gomez](/shorts/GYG) from 12.49% to 3.75%.

## Household-exposed stocks to watch

### Westpac (WBC)

- **What the data shows:** 3.19% short (109.1 million shares), first above 2.99% on 26 October. Close $36.81 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** its 2 November result (arrears above).
- **The case others make for it:** its pack shows arrears no higher than a year earlier and a CET1 capital ratio of 12.5%.
- **The case others make against it:** the same pack expects house prices to fall 10.9% from March 2026 into 2027.
- **Next dated event:** the file for 5 November, its ex-dividend date, due 11 November; AGM 16 December ([calendar](https://www.westpac.com.au/about-westpac/investor-centre/financial-information/financial-calendar/)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether the position hedges capital notes or an index trade.

### Zip Co (ZIP)

- **What the data shows:** 13.95% short (173.8 million shares), from 15.84% on 23 October. Close $2.04.
- **What happened:** the 23 October update, above.
- **The case others make for it:** Zip reconfirmed FY27 cash EBTDA guidance of $340 million; three directors bought $400,665 of shares on market, 26 to 28 October (Appendix 3Y notices for [Andrew Stevens](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261028/pdf/075wjh63q7frjz.pdf), [Sarah Adam-Gedge](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261029/pdf/075y6pngcws8jn.pdf) and [Matthew Schuyler](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261030/pdf/07608pxqbgv569.pdf)).
- **The case others make against it:** net bad debts rose to 1.97% of volume from 1.65% a year earlier.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 19 November, which votes on a five-for-one share consolidation that would change share counts and the share price but not the percentage short ([notice](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261016/pdf/075d8g0d984y2b.pdf)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether the covering since 23 October is profit-taking or a change of view.

### Harvey Norman (HVN)

- **What the data shows:** 3.35% short (41.7 million shares), unchanged from 2 October. Close $4.43.
- **What happened:** FY26 profit after tax rose 2.0% to $528.5 million ([28 August](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260828/pdf/073cyxb0r7bjwz.pdf)).
- **The case others make for it:** Gerald Harvey, through the Harvey 2014 Share Trust, bought 463,000 shares on market for $1.90 million on 18 September ([Appendix 3Y](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260923/pdf/074dqdzx3vpq3c.pdf)).
- **The case others make against it:** slowing furnishings spending and falling home sales, above.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 25 November ([notice](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261022/pdf/075mmj1gctrgwp.pdf)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** which part of the business, if any, a position targets.

### Nick Scali (NCK)

- **What the data shows:** 6.72% short (5.7 million shares), from 7.33% on 22 October. Close $13.78.
- **What happened:** the 21 October update guided to underlying first-half profit of $28 million to $32 million.
- **The case others make for it:** UK orders rose 29%, and FY26 group profit after tax rose 22% ([7 August](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260807/pdf/072hysqzb4d8dk.pdf)).
- **The case others make against it:** local orders fell 15% after rising 11% a year earlier.
- **Next dated event:** no date yet for the half-year result; the FY26 one came on 13 February 2026.
- **What this cannot tell you:** who sold short into the update, or why.

### Vicinity Centres (VCX)

- **What the data shows:** 5.01% short (234.6 million securities), against 1.61% a year earlier. Close $2.42.
- **What happened:** the David Jones administration, above.
- **The case others make for it:** occupancy of 99.6%, and FY27 guidance reaffirmed on 28 October.
- **The case others make against it:** no bank guarantees, and that guidance now under review.
- **Next dated event:** none set; Vicinity will update when it can assess the effect.
- **What this cannot tell you:** why a build that predates the appointment was opened.

### PEXA (PXA)

- **What the data shows:** 5.79% short (10.2 million shares), against 2.25% a year earlier. Close $7.30.
- **What happened:** the 28 August guidance, above.
- **The case others make for it:** FY26 revenue rose 7% and NPATA 35%.
- **The case others make against it:** FY27 guidance for group NPAT of $5 million to $20 million, against $26.3 million in FY26, and IPART's July draft proposing a 20% cut to PEXA's regulated revenue from FY28.
- **Next dated event:** the AGM on 11 November ([notice](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261009/pdf/07541ckj0zwwhl.pdf)).
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether October's covering reflects a changed view or a rising price.

## What the data cannot tell you

- **Who or why.** ASIC's file carries no names or reasons, and positions below the reporting threshold are not in it.
- **Anything after 3 November**, including the ex-dividend dates, David Jones and November sentiment.

<Callout type="warning" title="A position is a level, not a forecast">

Every position here comes from ASIC's daily aggregate, published four trading days after the position date. Each is the total of the short positions reported in a stock on one day; holders, motives and hedges are not in the file. Rising bank shorts beside flat arrears fit a view on credit, a hedge, or both. This analysis is not a recommendation.
