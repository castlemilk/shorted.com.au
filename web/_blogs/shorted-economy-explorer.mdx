# Australia's Economy by State

Shorted's [New South Wales economy page](/economy/nsw) puts the state's official figures beside the ASX companies that operate there, and tracks how heavily they are shorted. On Shorted's exposure-weighted measure, short interest across those companies was 2.53% of shares on issue in October 2026, up from 1.03% in October 2025. Four banks, [CBA](/shorts/CBA), [WBC](/shorts/WBC), [NAB](/shorts/NAB) and [ANZ](/shorts/ANZ), carry 30% of the state's weight, and each bank's reported short position at least doubled over the year.

The economy sits on the same page. On 10 November 2026 it showed unemployment of 4.7% in September and construction work done of $24.1 billion in the June quarter. In its [November Statement on Monetary Policy](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/smp/2026/nov/outlook.html), the Reserve Bank puts most of the data-centre investment pipeline in New South Wales and Victoria, though no series in the explorer separates data centres from other construction. The economy explorer has a page like this for every state and territory, and a hub for the country.

It is free, needs no account and has been live since 21 July 2026, at **[shorted.com.au/economy](/economy)**.

### How the short-interest line is built

The line is Shorted's calculation, not an ABS or RBA statistic. For each month it takes every company's last reported short position that month, from ASIC's daily reports, and weights it by two things: Shorted's estimate of the share of the company's operations in the state, and the company's market value. An AI model made those estimates for about 300 of the largest listed companies, from each company's name, industry and a short profile held by Shorted. Other companies count in full in the state of their registered office, marked HQ.

Three things follow:

- **Today's weights run backwards.** Current weights and market values are applied to every month back to January 2015, so the line shows how today's NSW-weighted companies were shorted over time. The page calls this "current-constituent weighting".
- **Large companies dominate.** Commonwealth Bank alone carries 14.1% of the NSW weight.
- **Small states and territories rest on a few names.** The ACT, Tasmania and the Northern Territory each have six or seven companies with at least a fifth of their operations there. Electro Optic Systems ([EOS](/shorts/EOS)) carries 39% of the ACT's weight, and its reported short position went from 0.15% of shares on issue on 4 November 2025 to 11.85% on 3 November 2026. By our arithmetic, that one position accounts for most of the ACT line's rise. The Lottery Corporation ([TLC](/shorts/TLC)) has an estimated tenth of its operations in Tasmania, yet carries 35% of the state's weight.

## How to read it

- **The cash-rate tile is a monthly average.** It reads 4.60% for October from the RBA's table F1.1, a monthly average of the target. The RBA raised the target to 4.85% on 3 November, effective 4 November, on a six to three vote ([RBA, 3 November 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-30.html)). November's average, expected with the 5 December run, includes the first days of November at 4.60%, so it will sit a little under 4.85%. For the current target, use the [RBA's cash rate page](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/cash-rate/).
- **It refreshes monthly.** The collector runs at 17:00 UTC on the 5th, early on the 6th in Sydney. The September-quarter Wage Price Index (18 November) and October's Labour Force (19 November), CPI (25 November) and household spending (30 November) wait for the 5 December run. Daily series stop at the run: the AUD/USD tile reads 0.7014, the RBA's rate for 5 November, and its rate for 9 November was 0.6894 ([table F11.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/f11.1-data.csv)).
- **Retail turnover stops at June 2025.** The ABS ceased [Retail Trade](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/retail-and-wholesale-trade/retail-trade-australia/latest-release) after June 2025, but the state pages still chart its history. Use household spending, which the ABS says covers around 68% of household consumption against Retail Trade's 33%. It is in current prices, so a rise can reflect higher prices rather than more purchases.
- **The NT and the ACT have no labour pages.** The ABS publishes no seasonally adjusted labour series for them, and a topic page needs at least two series: hence 70 topic pages, not 72.
- **A correlation is not a cause.** The NSW page's strongest chip pairs the line with household spending per person, r = 0.86 over 24 months, and both rose over those two years. The page's note reads "Correlations are descriptive, not causal".

## Decisions behind it

**Series keys come from codes, never labels.** Each series is named from the publisher's stable codes or a fixed map. Release wording changes, and a key built from it would quietly fork the history.

**A source that fails keeps its last good data.** A source that returns no observations counts as an error, not an empty month, and the run is marked degraded while the other sources still load.

**A failed read falls back to the last good copy.** Pages rebuild at most hourly. If Shorted's API fails during a rebuild, the page uses the last good copy of its data, kept for six hours. With none, its empty page is kept for one minute, not an hour.

<Callout type="warning" title="State short interest is Shorted's estimate, not a statistic">

The state lines and company lists rest on Shorted's AI-made estimates of where companies operate, with today's weights applied to every past month. In the ACT, Tasmania and the Northern Territory one or two companies can move the line. A reported short position is a level, not a flow: it can be a hedge, and it says nothing about who holds it or why. None of this is a recommendation.
