# Most shorted ASX stocks, October 2026: who is under fire

[DroneShield](/shorts/DRO) starts October as the most shorted company on the ASX, with 15.07% of its shares on issue reported short at 30 September 2026. Behind it sit two uranium stocks, a titanium metals company, a buy-now-pay-later company and a lithium miner on a twelve-week run of increases.

This is the dated October 2026 edition: the top ten, who built and who covered over the four weeks to 30 September, the sector shape, and the positions that would take weeks of ordinary trading to unwind. The framework (what a reported position is, why the denominator is issued capital) is in the [July 2026 edition](/blog/most-shorted-asx-stocks); this piece is the numbers.

Short-position figures are from ASIC's daily aggregate file for the position date 30 September 2026, published four trading days later; four-week changes compare it with the 2 September file, and ETF units are excluded. Prices (the 5 October closes), one-month price changes and days to cover are Shorted's figures.

What moved between the 2 September and 30 September files:

- **[DRO](/shorts/DRO)** kept first place as its position eased from 15.71% to 15.07%; the stock fell 7.62% in the week to 28 September. On 30 September the company announced a three-year indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract with the US Joint Interagency Task Force 401, ceiling value US$500 million. [The Motley Fool's note that morning](https://www.fool.com.au/2026/09/30/why-is-the-droneshield-share-price-surging-9-on-wednesday/) had the shares up 8.98% at $1.76 and quoted the company that the ceiling is not guaranteed revenue and no orders are locked in.
- **[BOE](/shorts/BOE)** is the largest build inside the ten, 9.91% to 13.58% (+3.67pp), with the price flat at $1.495. [Kalkine's 1 October piece](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/energy/which-path-is-boss-energy-asxboe-taking-beyond-honeymoon) describes a closely watched Honeymoon ramp-up, a new feasibility study released with the late-August result, and a September-quarter report due within weeks.
- **[LOT](/shorts/LOT)** covered from 15.67% to 13.53% (-2.14pp), second to third. In the [July edition](/blog/most-shorted-asx-stocks) it was the 22.81% outlier.
- **[IPX](/shorts/IPX)** rose from 10.68% to 12.89% (+2.20pp) as the price fell 19.3% to $2.39.
- **[ZIP](/shorts/ZIP)** barely moved, 12.51% to 12.71%.
- **[PLS](/shorts/PLS)** went from 11.25% to 12.63% (+1.38pp), a twelfth consecutive weekly increase by the week 40 report's count; the price fell 26.9% to $3.72.
- **[DMP](/shorts/DMP)** eased from 11.98% to 11.63%, and **[4DX](/shorts/4DX)** from 12.32% to 11.60% as its price rose 13.1%.
- **[PDN](/shorts/PDN)** rose from 10.74% to 11.36%. **[WTC](/shorts/WTC)** entered the ten, 9.30% to 10.94% (+1.63pp), with the price down 13.2% at $32.73.
- Out: **[TWE](/shorts/TWE)**, 11.74% to 10.64%, now thirteenth behind [TLX](/shorts/TLX) (10.87%) and [LYC](/shorts/LYC) (10.68%).

Sixteen companies sat at or above 10%; [FLT](/shorts/FLT) (10.28%), [ELD](/shorts/ELD) (10.26%) and [EOS](/shorts/EOS) (10.15%) complete the group.

<Callout type="warning" title="T+4, and a position is not a flow">

ASIC publishes each day's aggregate four trading days after the position date, so everything here is at least a week behind the market. A reported position is a level, the net of every reporter's short in the stock that day. A four-week change of +3pp is the sum of all the opening and closing in between: it says nothing about who traded, when or why, and it is not a forecast.

</Callout>

[Bapcor](/shorts/BAP) is the clearest unwind, and the order of events matters. The price moved first: $0.455 at the 26 August close, $0.645 the next day, $0.875 on 4 September, which is why the [September monthly report](/reports/monthly/2026-09) has it up 85.71% over its month to 24 September while the month to 5 October reads -7.4%. The position moved second: 7.94% on 2 September, 1.95% on 30 September. [Kalkine's 1 October piece](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/value/is-bapcor-asxbap-still-a-value-play-after-its-sharp-rebound) ties the rally to the late-August result: underlying EBITDA above a lowered guidance range, net bank debt cut sharply, a large statutory loss on mostly non-cash impairments, no final dividend.

The other four covered while the price fell. The file records the level, not the reason.

## The sector shape

**Uranium.** [BOE](/shorts/BOE) 13.58%, [LOT](/shorts/LOT) 13.53%, [PDN](/shorts/PDN) 11.36% and [DYL](/shorts/DYL) 8.65% are four uranium stocks in the top 25, and the four-week moves split: BOE +3.67pp and PDN +0.62pp against LOT -2.14pp and DYL -1.05pp. Paladin's shares were down around 17% in the week to 16 September with, [in the Motley Fool's words](https://www.fool.com.au/2026/09/16/down-17-in-a-week-what-has-happened-to-paladin-energy-shares/), no price-sensitive news from the company to explain it. [Kalkine](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/energy/why-did-paladin-energy-asxpdn-shares-slide-this-month) attributes part of the fall to a research house trimming its Langer Heinrich production forecasts.

**Lithium.** [PLS](/shorts/PLS) has the longest run the week 40 report records: twelve consecutive weekly increases. [CXO](/shorts/CXO) (0.23% to 3.82%), [LIN](/shorts/LIN) (1.18% to 4.28%) and [ELV](/shorts/ELV) (3.79% to 6.58%) built beside it, all three with the price down between 13.2% and 34.3%. [Kalkine's 5 October piece on PLS](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/lithium/why-cant-record-results-lift-pls-group-asxpls-shares) describes record full-year revenue and output and a resumed dividend, and concludes that "the market has chosen to look through the result toward a softer price outlook".

**Defence.** [DRO](/shorts/DRO) at the top and [EOS](/shorts/EOS) at 10.15% (+2.12pp) are the two defence stocks above 10%. EOS built while its price rose 25.4%: on 25 September the shares rose 7.21% to $11.45 after its R400 remote weapon system was listed on the US Joint Interagency Task Force 401 counter-drone marketplace, a listing [the Motley Fool noted](https://www.fool.com.au/2026/09/25/eos-shares-jump-7-as-asx-200-falls-could-15-be-next/) "doesn't represent a new contract". EOS tops the [squeeze radar](/battlegrounds) at 84.4; that score is Shorted's own (days to cover, short interest, price momentum, crowding), not an official statistic and not a forecast.

**The lottery rotation.** [TLC](/shorts/TLC) went from 6.23% to 8.63% (+2.40pp) while [JIN](/shorts/JIN) went from 7.64% to 2.32% (-5.32pp). TLC's position has doubled in share terms since 5 August, from 95,573,729 shares to 192,019,319, with shares on issue unchanged and 18.4 days of average volume to cover. [Kalkine's 24 September piece](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/midcap/are-lottery-corporation-shares-adapting-to-keno-curbs-asxtlc) sets out the backdrop: the federal gambling reform package passed in August prohibits online Keno and bans foreign-matched lotteries from the start of next year, and management has called the lost online Keno earnings a modest drag on EBITDA.

## What to do with it

The [crowded-short breakout](/picks/crowded-short-breakout) strategy takes positions of 5% or more and waits for a close above the prior 40-session high on heavy volume; nothing here qualifies by being crowded alone. [Battlegrounds](/battlegrounds) ranks the stocks where position and price pull against each other, and the [week 40 report](/reports/weekly/10-most-shorted-asx-stocks-week-40-2026) carries the weekly detail.
