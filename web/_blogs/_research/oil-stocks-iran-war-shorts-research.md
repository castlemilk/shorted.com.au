## 1. Executive Summary

The Israel-Iran / US-Iran war that escalated in early 2026 has created the largest oil supply shock in modern history. The Strait of Hormuz — through which roughly 20% of global oil and ~19-25% of global LNG flows — was effectively closed to commercial shipping for several months. Brent crude surged from around US$72/bbl in late February 2026 to peaks above US$115-126/bbl in April 2026, before falling back to roughly US$75-82/bbl by late June as US-Iran peace talks progressed.

For ASX energy investors, the conflict produced a classic volatility trade:
- **Winners in the spike:** Woodside (WDS), Santos (STO), Karoon (KAR) and, initially, Beach Energy (BPT) rallied 10-30%+.
- **Losers on the unwind:** When ceasefire hopes emerged in April and June, energy stocks sold off sharply — the ASX 200 Energy Index fell ~16% from its 7 April peak.
- **Short-interest angle:** Short sellers have not uniformly piled into energy. **Beach Energy (BPT)** is the standout, with short interest near **9.8-10%** of issued capital — among the highest on the ASX. Woodside and Santos remain lightly shorted at **~2.8%** and **~0.4%** respectively. This suggests the shorts are targeting company-specific weakness (BPT's production downgrades, domestic gas exposure) rather than making a macro bet against oil.

**Core article thesis options:**
1. **Contrarian / short-squeeze angle:** BPT is heavily shorted even after the oil shock; if Hormuz tensions flare again or the company stabilises operations, shorts could be forced to cover.
2. **Macro rotation angle:** The Iran war created a temporary "safe haven" bid for Australian LNG producers, but the trade is fragile and dependent on ceasefire headlines.
3. **Sector dispersion angle:** Not all ASX energy stocks are equal — LNG exporters (WDS, STO) behave differently to domestic gas/oil plays (BPT) and integrated utilities (ORG).
4. **Regulatory / data angle:** ASIC's T+4 short-position reporting reveals how professional positioning evolves during geopolitical crises.

## 2. Background: Short Selling on the ASX

### How ASIC short-position data works
- **Covered short selling** is permitted under the Corporations Act 2001; **naked short selling** is generally prohibited.
- Brokers must report daily gross short-sale volumes to the market operator.
- Short sellers must report **net short positions** to ASIC when they exceed the "small position" threshold: **A$100,000 in value AND 0.01% of securities on issue** (whichever is less).
- First report due within **3 reporting days**; continuing reports due **daily** while above threshold.
- ASIC publishes aggregated data on a **T+4 basis**.
- Sources: ASIC Regulatory Guide 196, ASIC Short Position Reports Table.

### What high short interest means
High short interest can signal:
- Negative sentiment or a bearish thesis
- Hedging (e.g., options market-making, convertible arbitrage, DRP underwriting)
- Index arbitrage or pairs trades
- Liquidity constraints
- A crowded trade that may reverse quickly (short-squeeze risk)

Important caveat from FNArena: "High short interest may include benign hedging... and does not necessarily equal a bearish naked short."

## 8. How the Iran War Impacts Short Positions — Key Narratives

### Narrative 1: Shorts are betting against operational weakness, not oil
BPT's 9.8% short interest appears driven by:
- Production guidance downgrade (FY26 revised to 19.4-20.3 MMboe)
- Domestic gas price weakness relative to global LNG
- Smaller scale and higher operational risk
- Dividend cut (interim dividend down 67%)

Even as oil prices surged, BPT underperformed peers — shorts were rewarded for the operational thesis, not the macro call.

### Narrative 2: LNG exporters are treated as geopolitical safe havens
WDS and STO are lightly shorted because:
- Their cargoes do not transit Hormuz
- Long-term contracts with Asian buyers provide cash-flow visibility
- Australia is a net energy exporter, potentially benefiting from higher prices
- Low production costs and strong balance sheets

### Narrative 3: Short covering during spikes, shorting on ceasefire headlines
Karoon Energy is the clearest example: short interest fell from 9.91% (27 Feb) to 3.55% (22 Jun) as oil prices surged, suggesting significant short covering during the rally. When peace talks advanced in June, energy stocks sold off — creating potential for shorts to re-enter.

### Narrative 4: The macro transmission channel
Even for energy stocks, the Iran war's impact flows through multiple channels:
1. **Oil/LNG price** → revenue and earnings
2. **Inflation expectations** → central bank policy (RBA, Fed)
3. **Risk premium / equity multiples** → discount rates
4. **Recession fears** → demand destruction if prices stay high
5. **USD and AUD dynamics** → translation of US-dollar commodity prices

### Narrative 5: Hedge funds and institutional positioning
- Money managers added **16,056 net-long WTI contracts** in the week ended 10 Jun 2025, taking total bullish bets to **179,134 contracts** (highest since January).
- Brent net-longs hit a 10-week high before the June 2025 strikes.
- In March 2026, hedge funds reportedly used exotic hybrid options to trade cross-asset volatility.
- Goldman Sachs noted global hedge funds "aggressively shorted" financial stocks while rotating away from US equity ETFs during the conflict.
