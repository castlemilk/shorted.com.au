# One stock page, seven tabs

[Westpac's](/shorts/WBC) page on Shorted opens on one line of numbers. On 10 November 2026 it read 3.19% short interest, up 0.34 percentage points in 30 days, with 109.1 million shares reported short of 3.420 billion on issue, as of 3 November 2026. That is Westpac's highest figure in records that start in 2010, above the 2.99% of 10 May 2016.

The figure is ASIC's: its report for 3 November, published on 9 November, four trading days after the position date (T+4), lists 109,075,775 Westpac shares short of 3,420,303,305 on issue. Prices are closes to 9 November.

Every tab is free to read at **[shorted.com.au/shorts/WBC](/shorts/WBC)**, and every ASX company on Shorted has the same page. A free account adds five chart indicators and a few tools; Premium, at A$4 a month, adds short-interest alerts by email and Shorted AI.

## Seven tabs, one question each

The tabs launched on 10 October 2026, each at its own address.

### Overview: what is happening now

[The Overview](/shorts/WBC) opens on the price and short-interest chart, then an About card that restates the header in words, strips for strategy fit and substantial holders, a fundamentals paragraph and the three newest headlines. Westpac's readout paired the 9 November close of $36.81 with short interest of 3.19% at 3 November.

### Short interest: how today compares with the past

[Short interest](/shorts/WBC/short-interest) opens the chart on short interest alone; its Indicators menu draws a 30-day average for everyone and a 90-day average for signed-in readers. A history card gives the change over 30 days, 90 days and a year, and the high and low since 2010: for Westpac +0.34, +1.33 and +2.60 percentage points (the year from 3 November 2025), a high of 3.19% in November 2026 and a low of 0.33% in August 2021. Below sit a Bear vs Bull verdict, a composite score of Shorted's own, then court, regulator and press items from web research, and five industry peers.

![Westpac's Short interest tab on 10 November 2026: the one-year chart rising to 3.19% at 3 Nov 2026, above a history card of changes and the high and low since 2010.](/assets/blog/shorted-stock-pages/wbc-short-interest-tab-2026-11-10.webp)

### Strategy: how it reads on the stock picker's rules

[Strategy](/shorts/WBC/strategy) runs the five strategies of [Shorted's stock picker](/strategies) over the stock, with each rule's result and evidence, under a banner reading the S&P/ASX 200's trend (Neutral at 9,029.7 on 9 November). On prices to 9 November Westpac read Watch on all five: some rules pass, and it ranks below the setups. Crowded-Short Breakout scored it 39, 153rd of 508. Westpac's 3.19% fails that strategy's core rule of at least 5% reported short, while days to cover passes at 18.4 days, Shorted's figure for shares short over 20-day average volume. The rules are published, with [backtests where the data allows](/blog/asx-strategy-backtests), and a reading is mechanical, not a recommendation.

### Financials: what it earned, on what basis

[Financials](/shorts/WBC/financials) holds the latest result, key ratios, statements, filings, dividends and, where the ATO's data names the company, a Tax paid card. For the year to 30 September 2026 Westpac shows statutory net profit of $6.81 billion against $6.92 billion and diluted earnings of $1.99 a share, beside an automated summary of its 2 November results. Ratios are statutory: the P/E of 18.5 divides the 9 November close by that $1.99, while Westpac's diluted figure excluding notable items is 204.0 cents. Most margin and debt ratios read n/m, not meaningful for a bank. The 77c final dividend, fully franked, went ex on 5 November and is payable on 21 December, and the Tax paid card shows $2.66 billion payable on $8.88 billion of taxable income in 2024-25.

### Company: who runs it and who holds it

[Company](/shorts/WBC/company) opens with a research profile the card labels AI-generated, then director trades, substantial holders, parliamentarians who declare an interest, the states it operates in (estimates, linked to [the economy explorer](/economy)), similar companies and, for signed-in readers, a dossier of public-source evidence. Holders come from ASX notices read by [Shorted's ownership explorer](/blog/shorted-ownership-explorer): State Street at 7.76%, down from 9.42% on 12 May 2026, and Vanguard at 7.003%, up from 6.003% on 17 September.

![The Substantial holders card on Westpac's Company tab on 10 November 2026: State Street at 7.76% and Vanguard at 7.003%, then index fund and super option holdings.](/assets/blog/shorted-stock-pages/wbc-substantial-holders-2026-11-10.webp)

Director trades come from Appendix 3Y notices, and each row is worth reading against its notice. Westpac's newest, for 3 November, says "exercise options": 102,380 shares at $36.820, worth $3,769,631.6. [The Appendix 3Y of 9 November](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163657) says chief executive Anthony Miller's 102,380 performance rights from the 2023 long-term variable reward were exercised into shares for nil consideration. The $36.82 is the notice's value per share, and nothing was bought on market. Other rows have no figures, or a role where a name should be.

### News: what was said

[News](/shorts/WBC/news) lists ASX announcements and articles from Stockhead, the Motley Fool, Kalkine, Google News and other feeds, above an event timeline. Each card's Positive, Neutral or Negative label is assigned by software: a language model reads press headlines, and a keyword rule labels ASX announcements and stands in when the model fails. It is not the publisher's view. A 5 November article on Westpac's 77c dividend going ex was labelled Neutral.

### Community: what other readers think

[Community](/shorts/WBC/community) holds research threads (bull, bear, catalyst and news-reaction posts) and a Live Pulse of short reactions. Anyone can read them; posting needs a free account. On 10 November Westpac's had no threads yet.

## The chart: ten indicators

The chart has three views (combined, short interest, and price and volume) and six periods, 1M to MAX. On the combined view a corr chip gives Shorted's Pearson correlation of price and short interest over the period, and a Divergence switch shades spans where the two trend the same way. Five of its ten indicators are free to everyone and five need a free account.

## Sharing a chart that stays true

The Share button opens a dialog with:

- **A preview** of the 1200 by 630 card a pasted link shows, which you can download.
- **The link**, `https://shorted.com.au/chart/WBC?view=combined` for Westpac's combined chart, with Copy link and, where the browser offers one, the device's share sheet.
- **Copy for Discord, Slack, X, LinkedIn, Reddit and Markdown**: a line and the link, because chat apps show a link's preview but print HTML as text. X, LinkedIn and Reddit get the code as `ASX:WBC`, since they read `$WBC` as a US ticker.
- **Website**, the only tab with an HTML snippet: a live chart and a credit line.

![The Share dialog for Westpac on 10 November 2026: a preview card (WBC at $36.81, 3.19% short as of 3 Nov 2026), the share link, and tabs for Discord, Slack, X, LinkedIn, Reddit, Markdown and Website.](/assets/blog/shorted-stock-pages/wbc-share-dialog-2026-11-10.webp)

The link opens the chart full screen under a slim bar back to the stock page. On a phone held upright a button turns the chart on its side, since many phones keep rotation locked, and `?landscape=1` opens it turned.

A link can also keep a chart as posted. Three dates pin it: `asof`, the day it was posted in Sydney; `shorts`, the newest ASIC report it showed; and `prices`, the newest close it drew. For example, `asof=2026-11-10&shorts=2026-11-03&prices=2026-11-09` pins a Westpac chart to ASIC's report for 3 November and the close of 9 November. The share page, its card and the website embed then end each series on those dates, so the chart looks the same whenever it is opened, with a Show latest switch. The Share dialog's link carries no dates, so its chart is live. By default [Shorted's Discord app](/blog/shorted-for-discord) pins each chart it posts when it knows both dates, and a pinned card whose dates have settled is cached for 30 days.

## How to read a stock page

- **T+4.** "As of 3 Nov 2026" is the position date. ASIC published it on 9 November, so the page runs about a week behind the market. The report for 5 November, Westpac's ex-dividend date, is due on 11 November.
- **A level, not a flow.** The 3.19% is the total of every reported short position in Westpac on 3 November. Who holds them, and why, is not in the file; a short can hedge convertible securities, an index position or a pair trade.
- **Percentages move with share counts.** Westpac had 3,420,353,305 shares on issue on 4 November 2025 and 3,420,303,305 on 3 November 2026, so its rise from 0.60% to 3.19% is in shares reported short, from 20.5 million to 109.1 million. Elsewhere a buy-back or a raising can move the percentage with no new shorting.

Short interest is ASIC's aggregate, published four trading days after the position date: a level, not a record of trades, silent on who holds a position and why.
