# ASX Short Interest Alerts

Shorted's alerts answer one request: tell me when the short side of a stock or an industry I watch gets crowded, without my checking ASIC's file every day. You write the rule, such as "[NEXTDC](/shorts/NXT) above 10%" or "any Materials company entering that industry's ten most shorted". Shorted checks it against every new ASIC report and emails you when it is met.

The only input is ASIC's daily aggregate short position report, which ASIC publishes four trading days after the position date (T+4). Every email names the two reports it compares and says how old the positions are. No email can say who holds a position or why.

Alerts are part of Shorted Premium, A$4 a month, at **[shorted.com.au/alerts](/alerts)**.

## Start with one company: NEXTDC

On 21 October 2026 [NEXTDC](/shorts/NXT) cut its FY27 revenue and earnings guidance after Jemena suspended parts of the grid works for its M3 Melbourne data centre ([NEXTDC's announcement](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03154955); [the newsroom's take](/news/nxt-shorts-8pc-into-40pc-fall-jemena-m3-delay)). Its reported short interest was 8.31% that day and 9.68% on 28 October.

On 29 October it was 10.74%: 81,576,741 of 759,626,698 shares, 8,015,699 more than the day before. The number of shares on issue did not change, so the rise came from more shares being reported short. ASIC published that report on Wednesday 4 November at about 11:30 AEDT. A daily "NXT above 10%" monitor would have fired in Shorted's 11:55 alert run that morning.

In the next session, 5 November, NEXTDC closed at $9.85, 50.8% above the previous close of $6.53. On 9 November NEXTDC said that Ackland Investments No.1, the vehicle Goodman Group and Digital Realty own equally, had bought 75,215,528 NEXTDC shares, a 9.9% interest, between 3 and 6 November ([NEXTDC's release](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163616); [Goodman and Digital Realty's statement](https://www.asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/displayAnnouncement.do?display=pdf&idsId=03163466)).

The email would have told you that 81.6 million shares were short. It knew nothing about the buyer. An alert reports what ASIC's file shows for a date four trading days earlier. It is not a forecast, and nothing in it pointed to 5 November.

Short interest kept rising, to 11.14% on 30 October and 11.59% on 3 November. The same monitor would have stayed silent, because it fires on a crossing, not on a level. ASIC's report for 5 November, the session of the jump, is due on 11 November. [Our data-centre analysis](/blog/asx-data-centres-deep-dive) follows the rest of NEXTDC's story.

## How it works

A monitor watches one ASX code or a whole industry, as grouped in Shorted's company data (NEXTDC sits in Software & Services). It has one of three triggers:

- **Short interest above X%.** It fires when the previous reading was below X and the latest is at or above it, both rounded to two decimals. Staying above does not fire again; falling below and crossing back does.
- **Short interest rises by X points.** It fires when the latest reading is at least X percentage points above the previous one. A fall never fires.
- **New top-ten entry**, for industries only. It fires when a company is among the industry's ten most shorted on the new report but was outside them on its previous reading.

On an industry monitor, "above" and "rises by" apply to every company in the industry, and one email lists each company that met the rule.

Each monitor runs daily or weekly. A daily monitor compares each new report with the one before it. A weekly monitor compares the last report of a week with the last report of the week before, once that week's final report is out. A Friday position is published the following Thursday, so that is when a weekly alert can fire.

<Steps title="From a short position to your inbox: ASIC's timetable and Shorted's schedules at 10 November 2026, Sydney time">

1. Day T, the position date

   ASIC's report adds up the short positions that sellers reported for that trading day.

2. Four trading days later, about 11:30 AEDT

   ASIC publishes the report. Each of the 11 files checked, published from 14 October to 9 November 2026, appeared at 11:30.

3. 11:32, 11:37 and 11:47

   Shorted checks ASIC for the new file, then hourly to 15:15 if it is late, with an evening run as the backstop.

4. 55 minutes past each hour

   On weekdays, Shorted checks every active monitor against any new report. If its import of the file is still running, the report waits for the next hour.

5. About 11:55 AEDT

   The email goes to your verified address.

</Steps>

The 11:55 time is worked out from these schedules. It was not measured from emails sent.

## What the email contains

Each monitor that fires on a report sends one email, from alerts@shorted.com.au. A stock alert has:

- A subject that states the rule and the figure, such as "NXT short interest crossed 10% (now 10.74%)" or "ZIP short interest up 2.68 pts to 15.84%". Weekly alerts start "Weekly:".
- The company's name, logo and industry.
- The previous and latest readings, each with its own report day, and the change in points.
- A chart of up to 130 readings, about six months, with your level drawn as a line on an "above" alert.
- Shares short and shares on issue, so you can see whether shorting or the share count moved.
- The stock's most-shorted rank that day, among every line in ASIC's report with a reported position apart from deferred-settlement lines, and the change since the reading 20 reports earlier.
- A link to the stock's page on Shorted, and a line naming both reports that says "ASIC publishes positions four trading days after they are held."
- A footer that repeats your rule, with "Pause this alert", "Manage your alerts" and "General information only, not financial advice."

An industry email replaces the chart and figures with a table: code, company, previous, latest and change, or latest with rank before and rank now for a top-ten alert. It lists up to 25 companies and links to the industry's page for the rest.

## Alerts from Claude or ChatGPT

AI assistants connected to [Shorted's MCP server](/blog/shorted-mcp-server) can manage alerts too. A client that has not signed in sees one alerts tool, `manage_my_alerts`. Calling it starts sign-in: the client opens Shorted's login page, and once you approve the `alerts:write` permission, six tools replace it: `list_my_alerts`, `create_alert`, `pause_alert`, `resume_alert`, `delete_alert` and `list_my_alert_history`.

They apply the same rules as the web page: Premium to create or resume an alert, at most 50 active alerts, and email only to a verified address. One difference: `create_alert` refuses an alert until your address is verified, where the page saves it and holds the email. Each action counts as one call against your usage limit. The [connection guide](/docs/mcp.md) shows how to connect each client and lists the six tools.

## What alerts cannot tell you

- **The positions are four trading days old.** ASIC publishes T+4, and the email follows. By the time NEXTDC's crossing was published on 4 November, Ackland had already started buying.
- **Short interest only.** There are no price, volume or director-trade alerts.
- **Crossings, not levels.** A monitor created while a stock is already above its level stays silent until the stock drops below it and crosses back. A stock that hovers at the line fires each time it crosses.
- **The percentage moves with the share count.** A capital raising or buy-back changes it without any change in shorting, and a consolidation rescales both counts, which is why the email shows both. [Why short interest can fall without covering](/blog/why-short-interest-falls-without-covering) works through examples.
- **A position, not a reason.** ASIC's reports "will not contain short seller details", and a reported short can hedge a convertible note or another holding. [How to read ASX short interest data](/blog/how-to-read-short-interest-data) covers the basics.
- **Email only, to a verified address.**
