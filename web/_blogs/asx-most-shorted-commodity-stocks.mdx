# Most shorted ASX stocks: lithium, uranium and rare earths

Seven of the 10 most shorted ASX stocks at 3 November 2026 produce or develop lithium, uranium, rare earths or titanium, against three on 3 November 2025. [PLS Group](/shorts/PLS) leads at 16.92% of its shares on issue, with [Lotus Resources](/shorts/LOT), [Paladin Energy](/shorts/PDN), [Boss Energy](/shorts/BOE), [Lynas Rare Earths](/shorts/LYC), [Vulcan Energy Resources](/shorts/VUL) and [IperionX](/shorts/IPX) also in the top ten. Ten of the top 20 are from the same four groups, against five a year earlier. Both counts exclude an ETF line and [Glencore's](/shorts/GLC) ASX line, explained in the box below.

In dollars the banks still lead: at 3 November closes the ten commodity positions were worth about A$4.2 billion, the big four banks' about A$17.1 billion ([the rate-rise piece](/blog/asx-rate-hikes-banks-landlords-retailers) covers them).

Short positions are from [ASIC's daily file](https://download.asic.gov.au/short-selling/RR20261103-001-SSDailyAggShortPos.csv) for 3 November, published on 9 November (T+4). Prices are Shorted's closes to 9 November, price only. Dollar values, days to cover and group means are Shorted's calculations.

## Why short interest pools in single-commodity miners

Three features of these companies could explain a short; ASIC's file can confirm none of them.

- **Prices are set elsewhere, mostly in China.** Lithium carbonate trades in Guangzhou, Beijing approves battery-cell capacity, and China's commerce ministry sets rare-earth export rules. A single-commodity miner is one of the few ASX ways to take a view on that price.
- **Developers need money.** Lotus raised A$60.1 million in an entitlement offer and A$35 million in convertible notes, and PLS borrowed US$700 million in October. A short can bet on dilution or hedge a note.
- **Takeovers paid in shares create hedges.** An arbitrageur who buys Meteoric Resources and sells Lynas against Lynas's all-share scheme is short Lynas whatever they think of rare earths.

## The macro backdrop

The RBA's [index of commodity prices](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/frequency/commodity-prices/2026/icp-1026.html) rose 4.6% in Australian dollars in the year to October (preliminary): base metals rose 12.3%, bulk commodities fell 6.0%. In US dollars it rose 11.6%; a stronger Australian dollar explains the gap ([RBA table F11.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/f11.1-data.csv)).

The World Bank's October averages ([Pink Sheet, 3 November](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/74e8be41ceb20fa0da750cda2f6b9e4e-0050012026/related/CMO-Pink-Sheet-November-2026.pdf)) show the split: copper US$14,508 a tonne, the highest monthly average in the series and up 35.1% on a year; Australian thermal coal US$152.3 a tonne (+41.7%); gold US$4,105 an ounce (+1.2%); iron ore US$90.8 a dry tonne (-12.3%).

On 9 November the RBA's Sarah Hunter [said](https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-ag-2026-11-09.html) the AI build-out is likely to increase demand for "some critical minerals" ([the data-centre piece](/blog/asx-data-centres-deep-dive) covers that build).

## Lithium: shorts followed the price

PLS's reported short fell from 14.67% at the end of October 2025 to 6.36% on 7 May 2026 while its share price rose from $3.30 to $6.37, before peaking at $6.74 on 1 June. The rebuild began on 29 May, when the short rose 165.0 million shares on volume of 401.6 million, about 20 times the previous 20-session average; the file does not say why. It was 9.91% on 3 August, 12.73% on 2 October and 16.92% (545.9 million shares) on 3 November. PLS closed at $2.89 on 9 November, 57.1% below its June high.

Benchmark Mineral Intelligence's [Q3 review](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/lithium-price-reversal-gets-respite-then-deepens-q3-2026-lithium-price-review) (2 October) has spodumene concentrate, FOB Australia, down 23.5% in the September quarter to US$1,780 a tonne, after Australian mine restarts, an inventory scare and Beijing's pause on approvals for new battery-cell capacity. By 2 November Citi had cut its three-month spodumene forecast to US$1,200 a tonne from US$2,000, [Investing.com reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/australian-lithium-stocks-slide-citi-054534198.html), citing Bloomberg. [The newsroom's lithium take](/news/lithium-shorts-build-pls-ltr-cxo-price-slump) covers Liontown and [Core Lithium](/shorts/CXO) to 15 October.

## Uranium: shorts rose with the price

Cameco's [month-end prices](https://www.cameco.com/invest/markets/uranium-price) put spot uranium at US$91.55 a pound on 30 October and the long-term price at US$98.00, up 14.4% and 15.3% on a year. Yet in the month to 3 November Paladin's reported short rose 3.17 points to 14.64%, Deep Yellow's 3.12 to 11.92%, Lotus's 2.25 to 16.67% and [Bannerman Energy](/shorts/BMN)'s 2.96 to 7.10%.

Paladin's and Deep Yellow's builds followed company news. Paladin's [September-quarter report](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261014/pdf/075963dqtq1mr2.pdf) (14 October) had Langer Heinrich producing 1.00 million pounds at US$61.60 a pound, against 1.23 million at US$51.60 in the June quarter, and the shares fell 9.3% that day. Deep Yellow [deferred its Tumas investment decision](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261016/pdf/075d8cwrfyqhs6.pdf) on 16 October. Lotus is restarting Kayelekera on [new funding](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261029/pdf/075y83m5nsg0zx.pdf) and on 2 November [said its first cargo, about 145,000 pounds, had left the mine](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261102/pdf/0763w9fk4gh5n7.pdf). The inference, which the file cannot confirm: these are positions on the miners' execution and funding, not on the uranium price. Boss Energy went the other way, to 14.22% from 20.35% a year earlier.

## Rare earths: a record short beside a record price

Lynas's 13.95% is the highest in its ASIC series, which starts in June 2010. Before this October the high was 13.80%, on 24 September 2012, and a year ago the short was 1.97%. The [quarterly report](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261029/pdf/075xzndxqk7000.pdf) of 29 October showed revenue of A$310.1 million, the highest since the June quarter of 2022, and a record average selling price of A$102.1 a kilogram.

The latest build, from 10.68% on 30 September, followed the [all-share scheme for Meteoric Resources](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261001/pdf/074stfmyyvwykm.pdf) on 1 October: 0.0207 Lynas shares per Meteoric share, about 63.3 million new Lynas shares on Meteoric's fully diluted count (Shorted's arithmetic). The 32.9 million shares added to the short since 30 September are about half that. Merger arbitrage is one possible cause; the file cannot show it. On 9 November China kept its October 2025 export controls suspended until 10 January 2027, [SMM reported](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/CNTOCBN4QTSC2L3BEOVBYILPAZVOOIHVV6INVPY-china-extends-suspension-of-rare-earth-export-controls-until-january-2027). [Critical Minerals Journal](https://www.criticalmineralsjournal.com/p/cw44-who-is-paying-for-lynas-price-floors-rare-earth-premium), citing Benchmark, put ex-China NdPr oxide at about US$106.5 a kilogram in September, below the US$110 floor.

## Where the shorts left

Where prices rose and cash flow is visible, shorts fell or stayed small. [Whitehaven](/shorts/WHC) is at 1.55%, down 1.59 points in a year, and [Yancoal](/shorts/YAL) at 0.79%, down 1.86, with thermal coal up 41.7%. [Sandfire Resources](/shorts/SFR) is at 2.92%, from 4.05%, with copper at a record; [Develop Global](/shorts/DVP), at 7.77%, is the copper exception. In iron ore, [BHP](/shorts/BHP) is at 1.56% and [Fortescue](/shorts/FMG) at 3.29%, while [Champion Iron](/shorts/CIA) is at a 12-month high of 7.34% after a loss-making quarter.

## Resources stocks to watch

### PLS Group (PLS)

- **What the data shows:** 16.92% on 3 November, up 4.19 points in a month; 13.9 days to cover; $2.89 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** a [decision to build the A$1.9 billion P2000 expansion](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261016/pdf/075d39f6c6pwb5.pdf) (16 October), [US$700 million of 8.00% notes](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261026/pdf/075rgwbd98sgmg.pdf) (26 October) and a [realised price down 14% on the quarter](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261028/pdf/075vy4hpd7cv63.pdf) to US$1,809 a tonne (28 October).
- **The case others make for it:** PLS's feasibility study puts P2000's incremental post-tax net present value at A$2.7 billion on a long-term spodumene price of US$1,620 a tonne, and PLS had A$1,277 million of net cash on 30 September.
- **The case others make against it:** Citi's US$1,200 three-month forecast is below that assumption, over a much shorter horizon.
- **Next dated event:** the [AGM](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260914/pdf/0742pt49zgrztj.pdf) on 24 November.
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether it hedges other lithium exposure.

### Lynas Rare Earths (LYC)

- **What the data shows:** 13.95% on 3 November; 22.1 days to cover; $13.15 on 9 November, 40.4% below its 14 April close of $22.07.
- **What happened:** the Meteoric scheme (1 October), a record selling price (29 October) and China's extended pause (9 November).
- **The case others make for it:** Lynas says floor-price agreements cover over half its production and that customers increasingly accept pricing independent of the market index.
- **The case others make against it:** Lynas expects lower December-quarter output during a month-long shutdown in Malaysia, and Critical Minerals Journal notes it does not say how much of its floor book is Japanese or US state money.
- **Next dated event:** the [AGM](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260925/pdf/074jxps5bcpmnx.pdf) on 25 November; Meteoric's scheme booklet is due in December, its vote in January 2027.
- **What this cannot tell you:** how much of the build is merger arbitrage.

### Paladin Energy (PDN)

- **What the data shows:** 14.64% on 3 November, up 3.17 points in a month and below its 18.80% record of 20 August 2025; 15.0 days to cover; $8.27 on 9 November. Meeting the bottom of guidance needs about 1.37 million pounds a quarter for three quarters (Shorted's arithmetic).
- **What happened:** the September-quarter report (14 October), described above.
- **The case others make for it:** Paladin kept FY2027 guidance of 5.1 to 5.6 million pounds at US$44 to US$48 a pound and expects higher-grade ore to lift second-half production.
- **The case others make against it:** the same report put the quarter's cost of production at US$61.60 a pound, above that range.
- **Next dated event:** the [AGM](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261016/pdf/075dtwrdrft8q8.pdf) on 17 November.
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether it hedges uranium held elsewhere.

### Deep Yellow (DYL)

- **What the data shows:** 11.92% on 3 November, from 9.69% on 15 October, the day before its Tumas update; 13.6 days to cover; $0.835 on 9 November, down 54.6% this year.
- **What happened:** the 16 October deferral of the Tumas investment decision.
- **The case others make for it:** Deep Yellow says its current works are funded from cash, [A$144.7 million at 30 September](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261028/pdf/075w04h20xgpf2.pdf), and that an updated value case is nearly complete.
- **The case others make against it:** by its own account, current equity market conditions do not support a decision to build Tumas this quarter.
- **Next dated event:** the [AGM](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260916/pdf/0744fvl10l37tl.pdf) on 18 November.
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether it anticipates a raising.

### Champion Iron (CIA)

- **What the data shows:** 7.34% on 3 November, a 12-month high (from 3.66% on 15 July) but below its 7.51% record of 11 February 2025; 13.4 days to cover; $2.10 on 9 November.
- **What happened:** [September-quarter results](https://newsroom.championiron.com/2026-10-28-CHAMPION-IRON-REPORTS-ITS-FY2027-SECOND-QUARTER-RESULTS) on 28 October: a C$48.4 million net loss, a cash operating margin of minus C$8.0 a tonne and freight up 39%. The shares fell 17.3% on 29 October.
- **The case others make for it:** record quarterly sales of 4.5 million tonnes and a first sale of direct-reduction pellet feed; the September [Resources and Energy Quarterly](https://www.industry.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/resources-and-energy-quarterly-september-2026.pdf) (REQ) expects India's steel expansion to lift demand for higher-grade ore.
- **The case others make against it:** the REQ forecasts Australian iron ore at about US$88 a tonne in 2026 and US$82 in 2027, and Champion lists the expiry of temporary covenant relief among its risks.
- **Next dated event:** none set; its December 2025 quarter results came on 29 January 2026.
- **What this cannot tell you:** whether it hedges iron ore held elsewhere.

### Whitehaven Coal (WHC)

- **What the data shows:** 1.55% on 3 November, from 3.14% a year earlier; 3.3 days to cover; $7.20 on 9 November, 26.8% below its 30 March close of $9.84.
- **What happened:** a [September-quarter average price of A$226 a tonne](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261023/pdf/075nzzhc9xpcnl.pdf), up 20% on a year (23 October), and a Land Court [recommendation to approve Winchester South](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261102/pdf/0764717r2vjfls.pdf) (2 November).
- **The case others make for it:** chief executive Paul Flynn said "coal prices continued to strengthen during the September quarter".
- **The case others make against it:** the REQ's central scenario has thermal coal prices back at their level before the Middle East conflict by mid-2027, and net debt was about A$1.4 billion on 30 September.
- **Next dated event:** none set after its 5 November AGM; its December 2025 quarterly report came on 29 January 2026.
- **What this cannot tell you:** why the shorts left.

## What the data cannot tell you

ASIC's file cannot separate a directional short from an arbitrage leg, a note holder's hedge or a market maker's stock, and positions under the reporting threshold are not reported. A percentage can fall because the share count rose, as Lotus's did between 30 July and 20 August and Vulcan's in December 2025 ([the guide](/blog/why-short-interest-falls-without-covering)), and covering, a closed hedge and a position slipping under the threshold look the same in the file.
